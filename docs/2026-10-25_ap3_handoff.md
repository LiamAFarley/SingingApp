## 1. The ask in one paragraph

Implement phase AP-3: the three-task auditory screening battery that AP-3 depends on, the aptitude profile that surfaces it, and the production additions from §4.5 — plus four small coach-facing changes listed in §6 that the training plan needs. The purpose is that the learner's route through the curriculum stops being chosen by feel. Right now we cannot tell whether his bottleneck is auditory working-memory capacity or the availability of a relative-pitch escape route, and those two diagnoses point to opposite curricula.

## 2. The problem this solves

`docs/AP_Trainer_Literature_search.md` §7 routes learners by working-memory capacity: near-ceiling echoic retention (INM ≤ 27.5 cents, 3-back d′ ≥ 3.5) goes to the twelve-tone multi-octave naming curriculum; lower capacity goes first through Eguchi's chord colours or a Brady-style over-exposed anchor, plus Taneda errorless discovery, "to build stable chroma representations without triggering prefrontal analytical fatigue."

AP-3 is what makes that routing possible. It is currently unbuilt, so the coach is selecting between two opposite curricula on impression. The learner's own data is ambiguous: 12-class naming sits at chance (15%), a 3-note subset reaches 45–50% cold, a two-note contrast task collapses to 0% when feedback is removed, and colour work is inconclusive because its level-1 design sits inside one octave where brightness can substitute for chroma.

## 4. Scope A — the screening battery

Three tasks, all **no feedback**, all flagged `assessment: true`, all tagged `ap.screening` plus a per-task tag, all storable so the profile can trend them.

### 4.1 `pitch_memory_match` — Implicit Note Memory (Van Hedger)

New exercise kind.

- **Stimuli:** 27 sine tones from 471.58 Hz (A♯4 + 20 cents) to 547.99 Hz (C♯5 − 20 cents), roughly 10 cents per step. Tones at the odd-numbered steps 5, 7, 9 … 23 (ten of them) are the targets.
- **Trial:** a target plays; a ~2 s mask; the learner then recreates the target starting from one of four fixed outer bounds — step 1 or 3 below the distribution, step 25 or 27 above it.
- **Interaction:** arrow keys move one step, Shift+arrow moves two; each move plays the resulting tone. The learner submits when it matches what they remember.
- **Scoring:** store error in steps and in cents, signed and absolute. Report mean absolute error per run.
- **Reference lines** (shown, not used as a pass rule): later-successful adult learners average 2.75 steps ≈ 27.5 cents (SE 0.30); lifelong AP possessors 2.44 steps ≈ 24.4 cents (SE 0.19).
- **Trials:** 10 targets is the reference design; run at least 2 repetitions so the mean error is not one trial.

### 4.2 `span` — auditory digit span

New exercise kind.

- **Stimuli:** spoken digits from the speech folder (`speech_dir` / `resolve`). If the folder is empty, fall back to synthesised tone sequences replayed on the pad, and say which mode ran in the run notes.
- **Procedure:** present a sequence of length L; the learner types the digits, or replays the tones in order. Length grows while correct, and stops after two errors at one length.
- **Score:** the longest length recalled correctly (the standard span), plus the full length/accuracy ladder.
- **Reference line:** successful adult learners 8.83 digits (SE 0.65); non-AP musical controls 8.1; lifelong AP possessors 10.0.

### 4.3 `n_back` screening variant

Do not build a second n-back engine. Add what the screening needs to the existing one:

- an **absolute, keyless mode**: tones drawn from the twelve pitch classes across at least three octaves, no key context, no drone, no degrees;
- a **spoken-letter mode** using the speech folder;
- 2-back and 3-back runs with **d′ reported per run** (the existing d′ metric path is fine);
- reference lines: 4.05 (2-back) and 3.52 (3-back) for successful learners; 3.37 and 2.27 for undergraduates.

If the existing params already cover this by configuration (`keys`, `degrees`, `drone: false`), then only the letters mode and the reference lines need adding — say so in the commit rather than adding a parallel path.

### 4.4 Composition

- A preset (or a course with one level) that runs all three in one sitting, in the order INM → n-back → span, no feedback anywhere, under 12 minutes total.
- Must be queueable through `queue_session` with `dry_run` validating clean, and must carry the course/protocol tags so the profile can find it.
- Schedule it monthly per the plan (one run a month, repeats feed the aptitude profile).

## 6. Scope C — production (plan §4.5) and four coach-facing fixes

### 6.1 Production additions from the plan

- **Sing by label**: `sing_pitch` prompted by a colour swatch, a Gervain name ("Sarah"), or a spoken name, instead of the note name.
- **Hold and compare**: after the learner sings, the target fades in under their voice and the tuner shows the gap.
- **Production column in mastery**: a class counts as known when it is both named and sung reliably, not named only.
- **Internal anchor accuracy trend**: the existing "sing your C from memory" trial already measures cents-off; surface it as a trend line rather than as a course requirement.

### 6.2 A production course (the gap this leaves)

There is no production *training* in the app. `sing_pitch` appears only in the Brady anchor check (1 trial per session) and the 14-day benchmark (12 trials). With Brady turned off, the daily set contains no singing at all.

Add a production course with the shape the coach asked for:

- sing the target named in the prompt, from memory, no reference tone;
- 3–4 s response window;
- feedback after the trial;
- classes grow the way `ap_naming` grows (start with C, then add);
- report **cents error and settling time**, not class-correct alone;
- **must not presuppose an anchor C** — it has to work for a learner who cannot yet hold any note.

### 6.3 The four small fixes

1. **Daily-set ordering is not controllable.** `courses.rs:1493–1495` rotates the active ladder courses by `local day-of-era % count`, so the plan's stored order only sets the cycle, not the day's order. The learner wants colour and contrast work first and naming last, every session. Either drop the rotation so plan order is honoured, or add an explicit order field to `PlanCourse` and sort by it. Deleting the rotation is the smaller change and matches what the plan order is for.
2. **Per-class mastery ignores colour work.** `ap_dashboard` builds its per-note table from `pitch_id` trials only, so colour and chord trials — which store the pitch class — never register. If naming practice drops, the per-class table freezes and colour progress becomes invisible. Extend the query to any trial carrying a pitch class, and label the source.
3. **Contrast levels 1–2 are a two-way discrimination task with a degenerate solution.** With two notes, "the one that isn't the other one" is a complete strategy, and an inverted label mapping passes the training block at ~70% while failing the cold check 0/10. The learner has been moved to level 2 (E♭, F♯, A) by hand, where the degenerate solution stops working. Consider whether level 1 should be three notes, or whether the ladder should require a cold check inside the level rather than a separate block.
4. **`ap_speed` is dormant but still in the plan.** Leave it; it is switchable.

## 8. Acceptance criteria

1. `ap_screening` (preset or course) composes, validates with `queue_session` `dry_run`, and runs all three tasks in under 12 minutes with no feedback on any trial.
2. `get_aptitude_profile` returns INM error (steps and cents), n-back d′ per n, span, run dates, the mode that ran, and the reference lines.
3. The numbers survive a restart and appear after a second run in a trend form.
4. A production course exists, is listed by `list_courses`, composes into the daily set, and reports cents error and settling.
5. Sing-by-label and hold-and-compare work in the runner, with a test covering the label→target mapping.
6. Daily-set order matches plan order in two consecutive sessions (the second session proves the rotation is gone).
7. Unit tests: INM step↔cent conversion and error sign; span termination rule (two errors at one length); d′ against a hand-computed fixture; a validation test for each new block kind.
8. No regression: existing course levels, the four AP tests, and the relative-pitch sets all still compose and run.

## 11. Deferred to after AP-3: the colour course gets the octaves

**Decision taken 2026-10-26, to be implemented once AP-3 has landed.** Not part of the AP-3 scope above; do not fold it into that work.

**What changes.** The note-colour course becomes the primary AP binding channel, with Taneda colour labels over a spread register from level 1 instead of a single octave:

- keep the class pacing: two or three classes at level 1, one added per level, twelve at the top;
- spread the register immediately — octaves 3–6 — so the same class never sits at one height;
- keep the `Other` option and the out-of-set distractors that are already there;
- keep Taneda's feedback design: no error signal, misses silently re-queued, the correct colour shown rather than an error marked.

**Why.** `courses.rs` / `ap_courses.rs` currently put note colours in one octave until level 11, which makes height a usable cue for the whole beginner ladder: C is always C4, so "the bright one in the middle" answers without any chroma. The fixed register traces to Taneda's physical keyboard, where the keys are simply where they are, and to a population aged 2–6 who acquire AP anyway. Nothing in `docs/AP_Trainer_Literature_search.md` or `ap_research_integration_plan.md` justifies it for adults; the plan's §4.6 asserts "a fixed register" with no rationale and says only "later levels spread octaves". The strongest evidence in the same documents — Wong's architecture — puts octave spread and 13-semitone jumps in place **from the start**. Empirically the fixed-register stage has not paid for itself: several sessions at three colours in one octave produced 44% cold against 33% chance, with no category formed.

**The jump rule needs to be better than 13 semitones.** The learner can reason "about an octave up, then a third" across any jump that is close to an octave multiple, and `min_octave_step: 2` guarantees exactly that shape — every transition becomes an octave multiple plus a small interval. So for this course:

- require `|Δ|` ≥ 13 semitones **and** reject any jump within ±2 semitones of a multiple of 12 (12, 24, 36);
- the generator should redraw the octave, then the class, and record `constraint_relaxed` if it gives up, as §3.1 of the plan already specifies.

Consider applying the same octave-multiple exclusion to `ap_naming`, since it has the same weakness.

**Naming's role shrinks.** Once colours carry the octaves, the naming course has no unique training content — it differs only by its letter labels. Keep it as an occasional alignment and transfer check, not a daily channel, and let the benchmark (letters, uniform twelve classes, four octaves) remain the arbiter of whether colour-trained categories transfer to letter names.

**Do not add a between-trial random-tone mask** while making this change. It is banned in the directory instructions, it was removed in commit `3ccd96a`, and it does not interrupt working memory.

**Open questions for Liam before implementation.** Whether the exclusion should be ±2 or ±3 semitones; whether the jump should be randomised per trial rather than enforced as a minimum; and whether level 1 keeps a single octave for one session as a discovery pass, or spreads from the first trial.
