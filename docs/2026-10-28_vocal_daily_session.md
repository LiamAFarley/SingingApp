# Daily vocal session, 35–40 minutes

**Date:** 2026-10-28
**For:** Liam, and the coding agent where features are needed.

One session, run daily, top to bottom. Everything below specifies pitches, tempo, vowel and rep count so it can be run against a click and a drone without deciding anything in the moment. Where the app already does the job, the preset or course is named. Where it doesn't, the feature is named and specified in `2026-10-28_vocal_feature_requests.md`.

The straw work sits in section 1: six protocols taken from the feminization doc, not one exercise repeated. Nothing later reuses them.

## 1. Airflow through the straw — 10 min

Setup for all of 1: 3.0 to 3.5 mm bore straw in air, parallel to the floor. Inhale through the nose or mouth, never through the straw. Lips sealed gently, no perioral tension. Cue throughout: rib cage stays buoyantly expanded, abdominal wall moves inward progressively through the phrase.

| #   | Protocol                                 | How                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Reps                                   |
| --- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| 1.1 | Pure straw calibration and vowel shaping | Sustain a comfortable middle pitch through the straw, then alternate every other phrase between straw and open mouth. Inside the mouth make ee as in see, or make the lips together shape as in starting to say boy. Do not make mm as in hum, which sends air through the nose. Pinch the nose at intervals to check for zero nasal leak. Pretend the lips end at the far tip of the straw. Purpose is a clean baseline with no breathiness and no pressed attack. | 6 sustains, alternating straw and open |
| 1.2 | Variable-resistance multi-straw | Ascending 5-note scales, starting with two straws lengthwise or a single 3.5 mm straw, then two straws side by side, then three side by side. High resistance first gives maximum back-pressure so the folds stay thin with little effort, then each step removes support while keeping the same ease. Cheeks puff slightly as pressure builds. Attention on ease of vibration in the mask and palate, not loudness. | 1 pass per resistance |
| 1.3 | Bumps in the road                        | Two adjacent pitches a major or minor second apart in the middle to upper transition. Oscillate between them while swelling quiet LOUD quiet LOUD without breaking the air stream. Pitch must not sharpen on the loud swells, airflow must not stall in the quiet valleys.                        | 4 pairs                                |
| 1.4 | Burst-sustain onset                      | 4 rapid staccato bursts through the straw followed at once by 2 long sustains on the same pitch, on the same airstream. Bursts start from gentle abdominal engagement, no hard glottal click. Repeat across resistances.                                                                          | 4 rounds                               |
| 1.5 | Submerged bubbling                       | 5.0 mm straw 2 to 5 cm into water. Blow steady bubbles first, then a gentle hum at E3 to A3, then smooth siren glides up and down the full range. Keep bubbling steady and uniform, no eruptive bubbling on the upper glides.                                                                     | 3 times 5 s plus 2 glides              |
| 1.6 | Prosodic straw speech                    | Through a 3.0 mm straw, intone "What a great day!" and "I love vocal warm-ups!" with high inflection, letting pitch roam in the upper speaking register. Exaggerated cheerful affect, melody of speech drives the glides.                                                                         | 3 sentences                            |

## 2. Unvoiced breath control — 5 min

Purpose: train steady airflow and abdominal engagement without phonation, so pressure control exists before voice is added. Ragan 3.3 states the exercise develops control of the antagonistic respiratory muscles for optimal airflow without the added factor of phonation. Bickel states breath control in singing is primarily exhalation control with diaphragmatic-costal breathing.

Setup: standing with lifted sternum and stretched spine, shoulders down and relaxed. Inhale with outward abdominal release and active lateral rib expansion as one gesture. Ribs 8 to 10 widen sideways, no shoulder lift, no neck tension.

| # | Exercise | Pattern | Tempo | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Sustained /s/ | /s/ as in sit, one breath, airflow even from start to finish | free | 3 times 15 to 20 s |
| 2.2 | Sustained /f/ | /f/ as in fit, same shape, notice the wall engagement feels different from /s/ and whether airflow feels faster or slower | free | 2 times 15 s |
| 2.3 | Rhythmic patterns on /s/ and /f/ | short-long and long-short patterns, engage on each pulse and release between, detached not abrupt | 60 bpm | 4 patterns times 2 |
| 2.4 | Alternating pairs /s/-/z/ and /f/-/v/ | alternate each pair at one phoneme per beat, airflow even across the switch from unvoiced to voiced | 60 bpm | 2 rounds per pair |

Sensation: abdominal wall engages subtly at the outset then progressively inward through the phrase, ribs stay buoyantly expanded without being held out. Ragan states holding the ribs out creates tension. Bickel states the sternum stays lifted up and forward even when lung capacity is depleted, with the lower abdomen drawing inward at the end and intercostal effort holding the ribs out.

Focus: evenness of airflow and release between pulses. The release matters as much as the engagement.

Pitfalls: holding the ribs out produces tension in multiple muscles. Tensing tongue, lips or jaw changes the resistance and fakes the result, so keep them loose. Pushing the abdomen out on purpose or pulling it in abruptly creates excess subglottal pressure and tension.

Move to section 3 when 2.1 runs 20 s steady three times with no rib collapse and no sense of pushing. Ragan 3.5 states these coordinations come before further complexities of singing.

## 3. Onset and release — 5 min

Purpose: set pitch before phonation and release between pulses. Ragan states efficient voicing is coordination of fold closure, air pressure, airflow and tract shaping, and that staccato trains onsets and offsets with the abdominal wall moving at the same rate as the folds close and open. The release between notes matters as much as the engagement, and there is never a feeling of bearing down.

Setup: same posture as section 2. Start single-pitch staccato on /ba/ since the consonant avoids glottal challenges while learning the abdominal gesture, then move to vowel-initiated words with a silent /h/ image for balance between airflow and engagement.

| # | Exercise | Pitch / pattern | Tempo | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Staccato on /a/ with silent /h/ before each onset | C3 times 8, then G3 times 8 | 60 bpm | 2 sets |
| 3.2 | Staccato alternating | C3 to G3, 4 each | 60 bpm | 2 rounds |
| 3.3 | Detached five-note | C3-D3-E3-D3-C3 on /a/ | 72 bpm | 4 rounds |
| 3.4 | Measurement | app preset **Vocal: onset, land on it** | — | 1 run |

Target for 3.1 to 3.3: pitch set before phonation with precise pitch and no scooping, equal resonance throughout, released between pulses. Ragan states the staccato note onsets with precise pitch and equal resonant quality, progressing to triad and arpeggio once coordinated. Then 3.4 reads `attack_cents` and `correction_cents`. Small attack with small correction means the pitch was set, not slid into.

## 4. Resonance and placement — 6 min

Purpose: keep resonance free so phonation stays easy. Malde states the head must be balanced at the joint for resonance structures to function freely, with throat neither stretched nor tensed and jaw slightly open. Balance the skull without the jaw, then let the jaw hang as an appendage. Neck release indicates balance.

Each item runs against a drone on its working pitch.

| # | Exercise | Pitch / pattern | Tempo | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Hum to vowel | C3, E3, G3, A3, C4 — 4 s hum, then same note on /u/ | free | 1 pass |
| 4.2 | NEE placement, single notes | C4, D4, E4, F4, G4 on /i/, 4 s each | free | 2 passes |
| 4.3 | Vowel chain on one pitch | G3: /i/ → /e/ → /a/ → /o/ → /u/, 5 s each, pitch and level held | free | 2 passes |
| 4.4 | Twang A/B | G4 on /i/ plain, then with oral twang, then plain again | free | 5 notes, C4 up to G4 |

The reference point for 4.1 to 4.4 is the ease of section 1: if a vowel needs more air than the straw did, it is being pushed. Feel for vibration in the mask and palate with no extra loudness, tongue high and forward on /i/ with the jaw loose.

## 5. Weight — 6 min

Purpose: thin the folds without losing edge contact, before condensing resonance. The doc orders weight first because resonance-first pulls the folds heavy. Weight is fold mass per cycle and closed quotient. Light means higher open quotient without breathy hypoadduction.

| #   | Exercise                      | Pitch / pattern                                           | Tempo  | Reps                        |
| --- | ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | --------------------------- |
| 5.1 | Messa di voce weight gradient | F4, G4, A4 — swell to moderate, back down                 | free   | 2 each                      |
| 5.2 | Heavy-heavy-light             | mee-may-mah on G4-E4-C4 descending                        | 66 bpm | 3 rounds, third round light |
| 5.3 | Ascending speech-level | C4-E4-G4-A4-C5 ascending, speech-level weight throughout | 72 bpm | 3 reps |
| 5.4 | Light phrase                  | any 2-bar phrase from repertoire, sung deliberately light | free   | 3 reps                      |

## 6. Long holds as the support readout — 4 min

Purpose: read support steadiness, not drill vibrato. Malde lists six causes of vibrato trouble with uneven breath pressure among them, and states vibrato appears when the intrinsics meet subglottic pressure with the body springy. The entry point is finding where movement already occurs, usually a resonant pitch-vowel combination or the tail of a long note, then recreating those conditions. Ragan trains the airflow that makes that possible. No book here prescribes an oscillation drill.

No vibrato drill. Hold steady and record what happens. Vibrato that appears on its own, usually late in the note or on one pitch-vowel combination, is the support readout.

| # | Exercise | Pitch / pattern | Tempo | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Holds | 10 s on C3, G3, C4, E4, G4, A4, plain vowels | 1 pass |
| 6.2 | Measurement | app preset **Vocal: 10 s holds, stability and vibrato** | 1 run |
| 6.3 | Messa di voce, pitch steady | G3 and C4, 6 s swell and fade | 3 each |

Record for 6.1: which pitch, which vowel, roughly how far into the note anything started moving. Then 6.2 gives drift, stability and whether vibrato is present. If drift is large with the drone off and small with it on, the hold is a memory problem. If wavering stays high with the drone on, it is production.

## 7. Articulation — 3 min

Spoken, no reference track needed. Say both phrases at the light placement from section 5, quiet and forward, no added volume. That is the whole task: keep the placement while speaking normally.

Symbols used here are sounds, not letters. /h/ is h as in heat. /i/ is ee as in see. /f/ is f as in fit. /s/ is s as in sit. /b/ is b as in boy. /m/ is m as in hum, with air going through the nose.

"Heat from fire" works because /h/ starts airflow before the folds close, so no hard attack. /i/ puts the tongue high and forward, the brightest vowel, and anchors the rest of the sentence. /f/ keeps contact at the teeth and lower lip, which keeps the sound forward. Say it slowly three times holding that forward position, then three times faster without letting it drop back. If the /h/ turns harsh or the throat tightens, stop and reset on one straw breath first.

"She sees three green trees" is dense /i/ and /s/. Make the /s/ with the tongue tip forward at the ridge behind the top teeth, lips unrounded, corners slightly back. If the sibilants go dull or hissy the tongue slipped back. Same approach: three times moderate, placement forward throughout, then use it once in a real sentence.

| # | Exercise | Text | Tempo | Reps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | Phonetic chain | "heat from fire, fire from heat" | slow, then faster | 3 + 3 |
| 7.2 | Sibilant chain | "she sees three green trees" | moderate | 3 |
| 7.3 | Transfer | one sentence you would actually say, at the same placement | speech | 3 |

## 8. What runs in the app, and when

- **Vocal: onset, land on it** — after section 3, every session. Watch `attack_cents` and `correction_cents`.
- **Vocal: 10 s holds, stability and vibrato** — after section 6. Watch drift, stability, and whether vibrato is present and when it starts.

Both are saved presets. About 4 minutes of the session.

## 9. Progression across weeks

- **Weeks 1–2:** run as written. The change to look for is section 6.1 producing one pitch-vowel combination where movement appears without effort.
- **Weeks 3–4:** move the working pitches in sections 4 and 6 up and down a fifth. Keep 5.1 at moderate level.
- **Week 5 on:** once the C4 to C5 ascending line in section 5.3 is smooth at 72 bpm, replace it with a scale from A3 to E4 and back, 3 rounds, plus a messa di voce at the top of that scale.
