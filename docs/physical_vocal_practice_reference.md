# Physical Vocal Practice Reference for the Planning Agent

**Purpose:** Reference material for an agent planning physical singing practice that is not well suited to deterministic in-app supervision.

This document complements the software plan. The app can measure pitch, timing, F0 trajectories, some intensity patterns, and exercise completion. It cannot reliably infer laryngeal posture, muscle tension, breathing mechanics, tissue health, or whether a technique is physiologically appropriate from microphone audio alone.

The agent should therefore use this reference conservatively.

---

# 1. General principles

## 1.1 Treat singing as coordination, not strength training

More repetitions, more volume, longer breath holds, and higher notes are not automatically better.

Prioritize:

- ease
- repeatability
- efficient coordination
- gradual range expansion
- musical transfer
- absence of pain or strain

Do not frame vocal practice like progressive overload in a gym.

## 1.2 Distinguish different adaptations

A singer may have different limitations in:

- hearing the target
- remembering the target
- reproducing the target
- stabilizing phonation
- maintaining intonation
- coordinating register transitions
- articulating rapidly
- controlling dynamics
- sustaining phrases
- producing stylistically appropriate vibrato

Do not prescribe generic scales for every problem.

## 1.3 Use measurements as feedback, not diagnosis

The app can quantify performance but cannot determine from audio alone whether a singer has a medical voice condition or whether internal muscular coordination is healthy.

If pain, persistent hoarseness, or meaningful loss of normal vocal function occurs, demanding practice should be reduced or stopped rather than "corrected" with more drills.

---

# 2. Evidence hierarchy

The agent should distinguish:

### Better supported / core

- gentle structured vocal warm-ups
- semi-occluded vocal tract exercises
- breath coordination while phonating
- pitch-matching practice
- feedback-guided vocal learning
- gradual range work
- sight singing and auditory-motor practice

### Plausible / useful but less standardized

- resonant humming
- lip or tongue trills
- slow sirens
- messa di voce-style dynamic control
- register-transition drills
- deliberate vibrato exploration
- familiar-song pitch anchoring
- general mobility/postural work

### Optional / evidence for direct singing improvement uncertain

- respiratory muscle resistance devices
- specialized inspiratory-strength training
- heavy "core" training specifically for voice
- elaborate posture regimens

### Do not prioritize as vibrato or voice-development methods

- heavy neck strengthening
- heavy abdominal pumping
- deliberate jaw shaking
- rapid abdominal pulsing to manufacture vibrato
- forcing extremes of range
- maximum-duration breath-hold competitions

---

# 3. Warm-up framework

A general warm-up can be short and adjustable rather than ritualized.

Example structure:

```text
1–2 min   easy body/neck/shoulder mobility
1–2 min   gentle breathing + unforced exhalation
2–3 min   SOVT / lip trill / hum
1–2 min   easy glides or five-note patterns
1–2 min   transfer into open vowels / short musical phrase
```

A total of roughly **5–8 minutes** is a practical design choice, not a scientifically fixed optimum.

If the singer already feels vocally warm and comfortable, a long warm-up is not automatically superior.

---

# 4. Semi-occluded vocal tract exercises (SOVT)

Useful forms include:

- straw phonation
- lip trill
- tongue trill
- humming
- voiced fricatives
- gentle resonant consonant-vowel transitions

## 4.1 Straw phonation

Suggested sequence:

1. phonate gently through the straw at a comfortable pitch
2. sustain several easy notes
3. perform small upward/downward glides
4. sing a simple five-note pattern
5. optionally sing a familiar melody through the straw
6. transfer the same ease into an open vowel

Goals:

- easy continuous phonation
- no forceful airflow
- smooth pitch movement
- efficient-feeling coordination

Do not assume more resistance is always better. Straw dimensions and immersion/resistance matter.

## 4.2 Lip or tongue trills

Use:

- sustained comfortable notes
- small glides
- five-note scales
- gentle arpeggios

Then transfer to humming and open vowels.

Stop if the trill itself requires excessive facial or jaw effort.

---

# 5. Humming and resonant phonation

Suggested progression:

```text
easy hum
→ small pitch movement
→ hum + vowel
→ short vowel phrase
→ normal singing
```

The goal is not to chase a specific spectral target.

The aim is to find a comfortable, efficient coordination and transfer it into normal singing.

The agent should avoid claims such as "this resonance placement is objectively correct" unless a qualified teacher/clinician has given the user a specific instruction.

---

# 6. Breath management

Breath work should emphasize coordination of expiration and phonation rather than maximum inhalation.

Useful exercise families:

| Exercise | Goal |
|---|---|
| controlled exhalation | smooth, uninterrupted airflow |
| sustained vowel | continuity and stable pitch |
| legato phrase | breath/pitch coordination |
| crescendo–diminuendo | maintain pitch while changing intensity |
| phrase planning | complete phrases without rushed or strained breathing |

## 6.1 Controlled exhalation

Use an easy, comfortable breath.

Exhale smoothly without trying to empty the lungs completely.

Possible variants:

- unvoiced hiss
- voiced fricative
- easy vowel

Do not turn it into a maximum-duration competition.

## 6.2 Sustained vowel

At a comfortable pitch and moderate loudness:

- sustain briefly
- monitor ease
- observe pitch drift
- stop before fatigue

Long duration is not the objective.

## 6.3 Messa di voce

For more advanced practice:

1. choose a comfortable sustained note
2. begin at a moderate/easy level
3. increase loudness gradually
4. decrease gradually
5. keep the pitch center stable

Start with small dynamic changes.

Do not begin with extreme soft-to-loud transitions or high notes.

---

# 7. Range and registration work

Distinguish:

- maximum producible note
- repeatable note
- usable performance note
- comfortable tessitura

The agent should progress **repeatable usable range**, not simply the pitch ceiling.

Useful exercises:

- slow sirens
- five-note scales
- arpeggios
- register-transition patterns
- matched-vowel patterns
- soft/moderate dynamic variations

## 7.1 Slow sirens

Use an easy vowel or SOVT configuration.

Move slowly through a comfortable portion of the range.

Goals:

- continuity
- absence of sudden force
- awareness of transition areas

Do not repeatedly sweep through uncomfortable extremes.

## 7.2 Five-note scales

Start centrally.

Transpose by semitone only when:

- notes are repeatable
- intonation is stable enough
- effort remains acceptable

A single successful extreme note should not trigger range expansion.

## 7.3 Arpeggios

Useful for interval targeting and register transitions.

Track:

- onset accuracy
- settled pitch
- recovery after leaps
- consistency
- subjective ease

## 7.4 Register transition work

Use gentle patterns that cross an individual transition area.

Goals:

- continuity
- predictable coordination
- reduced abrupt breaks
- maintained musical pitch

Do not tell the singer to force a specific anatomical mechanism based solely on acoustic output.

---

# 8. Agility and articulation

Useful exercise families:

- five-note scales
- arpeggios
- turns
- short runs
- repeated syllables
- consonant-vowel patterns
- staccato/legato contrasts

Progress variables:

```text
tempo
interval size
direction
articulation
range
vowel
syllable
pattern length
```

Do not increase all variables simultaneously.

A singer who struggles with a fast descending staccato phrase may need a slower legato version before returning to the original articulation.

---

# 9. Dynamics

Train pitch and dynamic control together.

Possible sequence:

```text
stable moderate note
→ slightly softer
→ slightly stronger
→ small crescendo
→ small diminuendo
→ musical phrase
```

Measurements the app can use:

- note-center pitch
- pitch drift
- relative intensity trajectory

But absolute microphone level should not be treated as vocal SPL without calibration.

---

# 10. Vibrato: guiding principles

Vibrato is not simply deliberate rapid pitch oscillation.

It interacts with phonatory coordination, style, intensity, and training.

A practical classical reference often places vibrato rate roughly around the mid-single-digit Hz range, but the agent should **not** prescribe one universal rate or width.

Do not tell the user to:

- pump the abdomen
- shake the jaw
- squeeze the throat
- force rapid oscillation

These are not established routes to healthy natural vibrato.

---

# 11. Progressive vibrato exploration

This is an evidence-informed practice framework, not a validated medical protocol.

## Stage 1 — comfortable sustained phonation

Choose several comfortable pitches.

Sustain an easy vowel for approximately 3–5 seconds.

Do not intentionally create vibrato.

Observe:

- comfort
- note center
- continuity
- spontaneous modulation

Goal: determine what the voice already does naturally.

## Stage 2 — coordination reset

Before returning to sustained vowels, try:

- SOVT
- humming
- gentle legato
- small dynamic changes

Then compare the sustained note again.

The point is to discover which preparatory conditions correlate with easier or more regular oscillation for the individual.

## Stage 3 — intentional slow modulation

Only after comfortable sustained phonation is established:

- make small slow pitch movements around the center
- keep movement light
- avoid forcing speed

This teaches awareness and control.

A manually oscillated pitch should not automatically be labeled "natural vibrato."

## Stage 4 — musical transfer

Practice:

```text
stable onset
→ optional vibrato development
→ controlled duration
→ clean release
```

Vary:

- vowel
- note length
- dynamic
- pitch
- phrase context
- style

The end goal is controllable musical use rather than "maximum vibrato."

---

# 12. Interpreting vibrato measurements

Useful app metrics:

- rate
- extent
- regularity
- onset delay
- center drift
- sustained duration
- amplitude modulation

But the planning agent should use them descriptively.

Examples:

Good planning statement:

> The vibrato is more regular and begins later in sustained notes than it did four weeks ago. Continue comfortable sustained-phonation and phrase-transfer work.

Poor planning statement:

> Your vibrato is 6.1 Hz, therefore your technique is healthy.

The latter is not justified.

---

# 13. Vocal feedback strategy

Realtime visual feedback can improve pitch learning but should be faded.

A practice block can rotate:

```text
1. realtime pitch display
2. intermittent display
3. no display, immediate playback
4. no display, delayed review
5. transfer to repertoire
```

The objective is independent performance.

The agent should not leave the singer permanently dependent on a tuner.

---

# 14. Personalized reference-voice practice

Consider using:

- piano
- synth
- professional singer
- user's own successful recording
- pitch-shifted version of user's own recording

A user's own voice can sometimes be a useful imitation target.

Exercise example:

```text
record a successful comfortable G4
→ save as personal reference
→ later present that G4 as target
→ compare against piano/professional target
```

Use performance data to determine which target type works best for the individual rather than assuming one is universally superior.

---

# 15. Physical mobility and posture

General mobility may support comfort and endurance, but evidence for specific posture drills causing better singing is limited.

Keep mobility low-intensity.

Potential pre-practice movements:

- gentle neck range of motion
- shoulder rolls
- thoracic extension
- relaxed rib-cage movement
- jaw relaxation
- tongue relaxation
- easy standing alignment checks

Avoid rigid "perfect posture" prescriptions.

The target is freedom of movement, not holding the body in a fixed pose.

---

# 16. General physical conditioning

Cardiovascular fitness, general strength, and mobility may help overall stamina and posture.

They should not be sold as direct methods for acquiring:

- vibrato
- absolute pitch
- high notes
- resonance
- breath support

If the user wants a general fitness plan, treat it as a separate training domain.

---

# 17. Respiratory muscle trainers

Respiratory resistance training may improve some respiratory physiological measures.

Evidence that it reliably improves singing quality is much weaker.

Agent stance:

```text
optional
not foundational
do not prioritize over singing-specific breath coordination
```

Do not recommend expensive breathing devices as required equipment.

---

# 18. Session design for physical/vocal work

A practical vocal session can be organized as:

```text
1. readiness / comfort check
2. brief mobility
3. SOVT / easy coordination
4. pitch accuracy
5. range / registration or agility
6. dynamics / vibrato / specialty work
7. sight singing or repertoire
8. easy cooldown if desired
9. post-session comfort/fatigue check
```

The exact duration should vary.

A reasonable moderate example:

```text
5–8 min   warm-up / SOVT
5–10 min  pitch + scales
5–10 min  targeted technical work
10–20 min repertoire / sight singing
```

This is a planning template, not an evidence-based universal dose.

---

# 19. Vocal load management

The agent should vary the amount of phonation across days.

Possible day types:

```text
NORMAL
  full singing session

LIGHT
  reduced range/intensity + more ear training

LISTENING
  little/no phonation; ear training and audiation

RECOVERY
  no demanding vocal work
```

A difficult singing day does not require abandoning music practice.

Shift toward:

- ear training
- rhythm
- score reading
- audiation
- transcription
- AP listening tasks

---

# 20. Readiness and safety questions

Before demanding vocal work, the agent can ask or read app state for:

```text
voice feels normal?
new hoarseness?
pain?
unusual fatigue?
recent heavy voice use?
comfortable speaking?
```

Possible responses:

### Normal

Proceed.

### Mild fatigue

Reduce:

- high range
- loud singing
- long sustained work
- repeated demanding phrases

### Discomfort

Prefer:

- listening
- low-demand exercises
- gentle SOVT if comfortable

Do not "train through" discomfort.

### Pain, major voice change, persistent hoarseness, or loss of normal function

Suspend demanding practice.

Recommend appropriate clinical assessment if persistent or concerning.

The app/agent should never diagnose.

---

# 21. Hydration and breaks

Useful general advice:

- maintain ordinary adequate hydration
- take voice breaks during prolonged use
- avoid unnecessary yelling/forcing
- avoid training through clear discomfort

Do not present hydration as an instant fix for every vocal problem.

---

# 22. Absolute pitch and physical singing work should remain separate

AP training and vocal motor training interact, but they are not the same.

A learner might:

- identify a pitch correctly but sing it inaccurately
- sing a remembered pitch correctly but fail to name it
- imitate a target well while having weak absolute memory
- have excellent relative pitch but weak absolute labeling

The planning agent should preserve this distinction when diagnosing a practice problem.

# 24. Example targeted prescriptions

## Problem: delayed pitch stabilization

Try:

- isolated sustained targets
- slightly slower onset
- own-voice imitation target
- delayed feedback
- then interval context

Track:

- initial error
- settling time
- settled error

## Problem: high-note inconsistency

Try:

- lower transposed version
- SOVT glide
- five-note scale approaching target region
- repeatable submaximal notes
- return to repertoire phrase

Do not repeatedly attack the maximum note.

## Problem: descending fast phrase

Try:

```text
slow + legato
→ slow + original articulation
→ moderate tempo
→ full tempo
→ original phrase
```

## Problem: unstable sustained note

Try:

- comfortable vowel
- shorter duration
- SOVT reset
- moderate dynamic
- gradually increase duration only if ease remains

## Problem: vibrato feels forced

Return to:

- sustained easy phonation
- SOVT/hum
- legato
- small dynamic variation

Stop explicit oscillation practice temporarily.

# 25. Agent planning rules

The agent should:

1. identify the specific failing dimension
2. simplify one variable at a time
3. choose an exercise with a plausible mechanism
4. define what improvement should look like
5. return to the original musical task
6. record whether transfer occurred

Example:

```text
Observed failure:
fast descending staccato phrase

Hypothesis:
coordination breaks under combined tempo + articulation demand

Remediation:
1. slow legato
2. slow staccato
3. moderate staccato
4. original tempo
5. original repertoire phrase

Evaluation:
pitch accuracy + onset timing + user comfort
```

This is preferable to prescribing unrelated generic warm-ups.

---

# 26. What the agent must not infer

Do not infer from microphone audio alone:

- vocal fold health
- laryngeal muscle tension
- "proper support"
- exact register mechanism
- presence/absence of pathology
- whether a spectral feature is physiologically desirable
- whether pain is harmless

Do not make clinical claims.

---

# 27. Research-informed notes for the agent

Important themes from the underlying literature review:

- singing improvements involve several partly independent perceptual and motor adaptations
- feedback can help but should be faded
- SOVT, structured warm-ups, and singing-specific coordination have more support than heavy generalized muscle training for vocal outcomes
- breath work is better framed as regulation/coordination than as maximal lung capacity
- usable tessitura matters more than one-off extreme notes
- vibrato development appears related to broader coordination and should not be reduced to mechanically oscillating pitch
- genre affects appropriate vibrato characteristics
- exercise response varies substantially between individuals
- physical work should be judged by musical transfer, not by performing the drill itself

# 29. Relationship to the app

The app should own:

- objective pitch/timing measurement
- exercise execution
- score display
- adaptive difficulty variables
- learner history
- feedback scheduling
- AP protocols
- transcription
- repertoire analysis

The planning agent can use this reference to add:

- SOVT work
- warm-ups
- mobility
- breath-coordination drills
- range/registration exercises
- vibrato exploration
- physical load management
- repertoire-specific physical preparation

The key boundary is:

> **The app measures what happened acoustically. The agent suggests practice structures. Neither should pretend to see internal physiology that the microphone cannot reveal.**
