# Vocal knowledge and exercises: feminization, lightness across range, support capacity

Date: 2026-10-29. For Liam. Goals: vocal feminization, lighter tone across the full range for a low tenor/baritone, improved support capacity.

This document combines the books in `books/`, the feminization docx, the spectrogram and tutorial reference notes, and the app's diagnostics guide. Every protocol states setup, execution, sensation, pitfall and progression. Sources are cited inline so each item traces to its passage.

How to use it: sections 1 to 7 are knowledge. Sections 8 to 14 are exercises. Section 15 maps each exercise to the app: preset name where one exists, off-app instruction where the microphone cannot grade, feature request number where a capability is missing.

## 1. Acoustic and aerodynamic foundations

Sound starts at the folds and is shaped by the tract. The tract reflects energy back down to the folds during each cycle. Narrowing above the folds raises acoustic impedance toward inertive reactance, which pulls the folds apart as they open and draws them together as they close. The result is lower phonation threshold pressure, less constrained mucosal wave, and more efficient conversion of air into sound. Without that narrowing the same loudness costs more pressure and more fold collision.

A flow-resistant straw elongates the tract and lowers its first resonance. A typical closed tract sits near 200 Hz. Straws drop it to 150 to 190 Hz, the narrowest to 130 Hz. Registration shifts track where harmonics cross the first formant, so lowering it moves the mix sensation down into low comfortable pitches. Heavy voices feel turn and mix without shouting. (Feminization docx section 1, lines 4 to 6; Titze and Maxfield as cited there.)

Practical consequences used throughout: 3.0 to 3.5 mm internal diameter straw in air, 14 cm length, gives the most consistent resistance across tract sizes. Wider than 3.5 mm fails to generate enough back-pressure for many singers, especially those used to higher subglottal pressures. Five minutes through a straw transiently lowers phonation threshold pressure. Ten to fifteen minutes stabilizes the change so it persists into open singing. (Docx lines 7 to 8.)

Small straws at 2 to 4 mm create the highest intraoral pressure. With swelling or insufficient closure they are contraindicated. Fatigue means stop at once. (Ragan chapter 4 SOVT ladder.)

## 2. Weight, size, fullness

Weight is fold mass per cycle and closed quotient versus open quotient. Lightening means raising open quotient toward a smoother softer tone without tipping into breathy incomplete closure. Size is tract resonance. Fullness is the ratio between them. (Docx lines 88 to 93.)

Full balanced means weight and size match: large tract with heavy weight reads as unaffected masculine, small tract with light weight reads as unaffected feminine. Overfull is small size with heavy weight: saturated, buzzy, metallic, nerdy, duck-like, strained. Underfull is light weight with large size: hollow, dopey, yawn-like, Patrick Star. (Docx lines 91 to 92.)

Sequence matters. Raising the larynx and constricting the pharynx pulls reflexive glottal thickening, which traps beginners loud and strained in Overfull. The docx orders weight thinning first, then resonance condensing, then articulation and prosody. Swallow-and-hold is explicitly rejected: it recruits swallowing constrictors, risks tension dysphonia and gagging, and locks the airway. Voiceless panting drills such as Big Dog Small Dog and whisper scream require glottal constriction to be audible and carry false-fold strain into phonation. (Docx lines 93.)

## 3. Tenor and baritone mix across the passaggio

Laryngeal registration is muscle dominance between thyroarytenoid chest mechanism M1 and cricothyroid head mechanism M2. Acoustic registration is timbral shifts as harmonics cross the first formant. Formant locations span roughly an octave by vowel, so acoustic events move by up to an octave with vowel choice. Lower entry into transition tracks the third harmonic crossing above the first formant on open vowels. The upper shift tracks the second harmonic crossing above it, moving open timbre to closed timbre. (Docx lines 47 to 48.)

Below whoop timbre, hold vocal tract shape stable rather than widening the jaw or spreading the lips. As harmonics rise above the first formant the vowel migrates toward a more closed cousin without active closing: /a/ toward /u/ or /o/, /e/ toward /e/ or rounded front vowels. Letting this passive turn happen prevents trapping the voice in a raw heavy shout by raising the first formant with the jaw. (Docx lines 49.)

At the point the fundamental itself approaches the first formant, active modification starts. Whoop timbre is the call You-hoo across a street, energy concentrated into the fundamental, warm flute-like release. Dropping into it too early on close vowels or with a depressed larynx turns dull and provokes squeeze. Hey timbre is the call Hey, upper harmonics boosted. The working mix balances whoop warmth with hey ring. (Docx lines 50.)

Chiaroscuro whisper trains resonance tuning without fold wear. Every vowel carries an over-vowel high in the pharynx and an under-vowel out front. The whisper is internalized and low-effort, like an inward Darth Vader whisper, shaped with minimal mouth movement. Tuned with affects such as mischief, skepticism, disgust, empathy, soothing reassurance, the first formant bandwidth narrows enough to produce an audible pitch in the whisper. Matching those pitches while keeping the warm under-vowel present gives bright-warm balance across the passaggio. (Docx lines 51 to 54, Bozeman table lines 55 to 79.)

Voce faringea is adducted falsetto-dominant mix with lateral pharyngeal wall approximation at the faucal pillars plus aryepiglottic narrowing. It turns anemic falsetto into a tenoral tone listeners mistake for chest voice. Long-term average spectrum shows a third peak at 1400 to 1800 Hz at virtually chest-voice level, minus 51 dB versus minus 52 dB. (Docx lines 80 to 82.)

Bridging syllables force equilibrium. Bratty Nay-Nay or Baa-Baa on octave slides and arpeggios uses nasal /n/ toward the mask while bright /eɪ/ and twang keep folds adducted into head mechanism. Gug, Mum, Bub stabilize over-brighteners: /g/ demands posterior closure with a neutral wedge vowel, bilabial occlusion gives mini back-pressure at each syllable edge. Singing a hard phrase entirely on Mum or Gug recalibrates before reintroducing text. Curbing holds a mild moan-like restraint on mid-closed vowels to connect the first two bridges. (Docx lines 87.)

## 4. Support: appoggio, torso anchoring, suspension

Support is breath management, not maximal inhalation. Lung volume governs recoil, which governs subglottal pressure and level. At full lungs recoil exceeds target pressure and blasts air unless held back. Near empty lungs recoil vanishes and singers squeeze the neck to hold volume. True sustain holds the sternum comfortably high, inhales silently without clavicular lift, suspends briefly, and balances inspiratory muscle activity against expiratory abdominal activity with epilaryngeal inertance so airflow use stays low. (Spectrogram reference notes, sustain section lines 178 to 185.)

Ragan chapter 3 trains this without phonation first. Sustained /s/ /f/ and th/sh sounds 10 to 20 seconds with consistent airflow. Inhale with outward abdominal release and lateral rib expansion. Exhale with subtle engagement first, progressively inward through the phrase, ribs buoyant not held out. Holding the ribs out creates tension. Rhythmic patterns at 60 bpm train engage and release in succession. Alternating unvoiced and voiced pairs /s/-/z/ /f/-/v/ at one phoneme per beat coordinates pressure, airflow and phonation before singing complexity. Flow-ball baseline at 3 to 4 inch elevation, voiceless staccato at 60 per note detached not abrupt, voiceless messa di voce over the full breath cycle followed at once by a legato phrase so the gesture transfers the same minute. (Subagent extraction task 1.)

Bickel chapter 4 gives the same system with different drills. Hissing S minimum three reps, counting aloud 1 to 10 through 1 to 40 plus per breath minimum three breath cycles, card buzzing with a 3x5 card through short then longer patterns on one breath, sustaining lower rib expansion against a belt at the seventh ribs, lifted sternum with stretched spine. Sensation checklist on inhalation is buoyant posture, lifted sternum, sideward lower-rib expansion, slight forward abdomen, no neck or shoulder tension. On exhalation the sternum stays lifted, sideward expansion stays out, lower abs draw inward, with intercostal effort holding the ribs and no neck or shoulder motion. Overdoing to pain is explicitly warned against. Build slowly. (Subagent extraction task 4.)

Malde chapters 2 and 3 give the postural base. Posture as stasis is rejected in favor of balance, buoyant, springy. Skeleton carries weight, soft tissue stays resilient. Head balances at the joint without the jaw: balancing head and jaw as one unit misplaces the head high or low, the jaw hangs as an appendage. Neck release signals balance. Support breaks from tanking up too much air, keeping ribs out instead of letting them return, confusing the diaphragm with the upper abdomen, pushing the abs out against a closed glottis, belly imagery, sipping imagery that closes the larynx, column-of-air imagery for a 4 to 6 inch trachea. Exhalation is regulated release, not forced contraction. Gathering and lengthening of the spine must happen or support never works optimally. (Subagent extraction task 3.)

Spectrogram notes add torso anchoring and suspension drills. Lying flat or hands clasped behind the head removes clavicular heaving and shows lateral rib and lower abdominal expansion. Silent inhalation plus 1 second suspension, then steady S or Z 20 to 30 seconds with sternum elevated and lower ribs expanded while abs draw inward, level flat on a meter. Then 12 second messa di voce in 4-4-4 shape with torso anchoring and thyroid tilt on the decrescendo so the voice thins from M1 into M2 without cracking or going flat. Vennard protocol from Vocal Athlete: lying with a book on the abdomen watching it rise and fall, or book against a wall, inhale 5 counts, hold 10, exhale slowly 5, lengthening gradually, then quick inhalation with pursed-lip exhale as long as possible. (Subagent extractions tasks 2 and 5.)

## 5. Resonance: NEE, twang, velopharyngeal seal

Size condensing needs three actions at once: raising the larynx, elevating and fronting tongue root and dorsum, shrinking the pharynx. Conscious neck flexing strains, so the doc elicits them through target sounds. (Docx lines 123 to 124.)

NEE and palatal anchoring: lateral tongue edges against upper molars, dorsum high and forward toward hard palate as /i/ while tip rests near lower teeth. Repeat one vowel or word such as one one one or heat heat heat while scaling size large and hollow to tiny and miniature on constant pitch, keeping vowel identity consistent. (Docx line 125.)

Oral twang with aryepiglottic narrowing: megaphone shape with shortened pharynx, high-front /i/, elevated velum sealing the nose, narrowed sphincter without false-fold compression. Raises output from 67 dB neutral to 74 dB low twang to 88 dB high twang. Elicited through baby cry wae, taunt nyeah, race car neeyeow, then dialed back about 70 percent into conversational tone. Thumb under chin monitors pressure without downward bulging. (Docx line 126.)

Hypernasality fix: soft palate dropping against raised dorsum leaks energy into the nose. Speak while pinching nostrils; muffle or buzz against the fingers means leakage. Crisp double-g between /ng/ hum and /a/ or /i/ trains clean lift until pinched and unpinched sound identical. (Docx line 127.)

Ragan resonance work: chant-humming with /hm/ mapped as /a/ with lips lightly closed, lingering equal on hum and vowel for continuity; glide-humming ascending and descending thirds fifths octaves allowing chest to head as needed; stepwise /hm/ watching for excess jaw motion; Ode Faull sequence with vibrant hung into close /ng/ then /i/ with little tongue change; alternating /m/ vowels; bent-forward and lying-ball positions to release postural muscles that contribute to turnover, especially at the second bridge; chopstick between teeth for /i/ with arched liquid tongue; weird /r/ work for clarity and ring; Y-buzz with lengthened tract; twang through /nyae/ and /ng/ with cautions against bracing. (Subagent extraction task 1.)

## 6. Articulation and prosody

Heat from fire is the calibration mantra because its phoneme sequence locks every target at once. /h/ starts airflow before adduction, preventing hard heavy attack and establishing high open quotient from the first millisecond. /i/ arches dorsum high and forward for maximum formant frequencies, the brightest ceiling anchoring the sentence. Final /t/ surfaces as a glottal allophone that checks air briefly while the tongue blade stays high. /f/ with incisors lightly on the lower lip shifts focus to the extreme front and narrows labial exit. /r/ trained tip raised and lips unrounded prevents plunging into a dark cavity. Schwa and /aɪ/ test holding neutral and open-onset diphthongs inside the high /i/ frame without dropping into dark throat space. (Docx lines 130 to 160.)

Sibilants and vowel chaining: /s/ /t/ /d/ slightly further forward at the upper ridge with lip corners subtly retracted in relaxed smile, never protruded or rounded, shrinking anterior space and shifting noise spectra higher. Soften consonant-to-vowel transitions, extend vowel durations slightly for legato flow. Anchor the vowel system to high-front /i/ with chains such as mee mae mah moh moo while posterior dorsum stays elevated and the jaw refuses the dark lower corner. (Docx line 162.)

Prosody replaces volume spikes with melodic pitch excursions, gliding up on emphasized words while holding weight light and steady. Wider overall pitch range, upward or circumflex inflections at clause boundaries instead of falling into fry, frequent micro-pauses for buoyant cadence. Non-verbal sounds initiate from /h/ or /i/ posture in head-dominant mix with elevated velum so laughs and sighs do not revert to heavy baseline. (Docx lines 163 to 164.)

## 7. Spectrogram markers used in this plan

Weight reads as H1 minus H2, spectral tilt, and closure indices. Firm adduction with abrupt closure gives strong upper harmonics, flat tilt toward zero, pressed heavy tone. Thin edges with leakage give steep roll-off toward one, breathy tone. Normative speaking sits near tilt 0.35. Breathiness index ABI runs 0 to 10. Regularity marker CPPS is healthy at or above 14.47 dB sustained. Quality index AVQI above 2.35 flags dysphonic irregularity. Pressed onset shows a vertical broadband click with suppressed fundamental. Breathy onset shows a diffuse high-frequency noise cloud before harmonics with weak upper partials. Balanced onset ignites harmonics together with H1 and H2 within about 2 dB and CPPS above 15 dB. Register break shows smooth harmonics fracturing or dropping upper partials as the fundamental crosses 350 to 700 Hz. Messa di voce holds fundamental within about 5 cents while upper harmonics bloom on the swell and the 2.8 to 3.2 kHz ring persists into the fade. (Spectrogram notes lines 25 to 69, 178 to 189.)

Vibrato at 5.0 to 7.0 Hz with extent about half to one semitone reads as synchronized sinusoidal waves across harmonics, tallest on upper partials. Below 4.5 Hz with wide excursion reads as wobble from slack adduction or weak anchoring. Above 7.5 Hz with narrow extent reads as tremolo from squeeze or locked support. Flat lines blooming late read as delayed onset, a stylistic device in contemporary styles. (Spectrogram notes lines 112 to 128.)

Mechanisms M0 to M3: pulse fry, modal chest M1 with closed quotient above 50 percent, falsetto head M2 near 47.6 percent differing in closure and reinforcement, whistle M3 above about C6 with sparse harmonics and subordinate H2. (Spectrogram notes lines 75 to 84.)

## 8. Straw protocols

Setup for all: 3.0 to 3.5 mm bore straw in air, parallel floor, inhale through nose or mouth never through straw, lips sealed gently, no perioral tension. Minimum effective dose is 10 to 15 minutes for the change to persist into open singing. Stop on fatigue, especially with 2 to 4 mm straws or water depths that raise pressure.

1. Pure straw calibration and vowel shaping. Sustain comfortable middle pitch, alternate every other phrase straw and open. Inside make ee as in see, or lips-together shape as in starting boy. Do not make mm as in hum through the nose. Pinch nose to verify zero leak. Pretend lips end at far tip. Baseline inertance with no breathiness and no pressed attack.
2. Variable-resistance multi-straw. Ascending 5-note scales starting two straws lengthwise or single 3.5 mm, then two side by side, then three side by side. High resistance first gives maximum back-pressure so folds stay thin with little effort, then each step removes support while keeping ease. Cheeks puff slightly. Ease in mask and palate, not loudness.
3. Bumps in the road. Two adjacent pitches a major or minor second apart in the middle to upper transition. Oscillate while swelling quiet loud quiet loud without breaking air. Pitch must not sharpen on loud, airflow must not stall in quiet. Trains loud without adding mass.
4. Burst-sustain onset. Four rapid staccato bursts plus two long sustains on the same pitch, same airstream. Bursts start from gentle abdominal engagement, no hard glottal click. Repeat across resistances. Clean light onset without ventricular incursion.
5. Submerged bubbling. 5.0 mm straw 2 to 5 cm water. Steady bubbles, gentle hum E3 to A3, siren glides full range. Turbulence steady and uniform, no eruptive bubbling on upper glides. Massage that strips chest weight and smooths breaks.
6. Prosodic straw speech. Through 3.0 mm straw intone What a great day and I love vocal warm-ups with high inflection in the upper speaking register. Cheerful affect, melody drives glides. Transfers light head-dominant mix into speech.

## 9. Support capacity

1. Sustained /s/ 15 to 20 seconds three times, /f/ twice. Outward abdominal release with lateral rib expansion on the inhale, subtle engagement first then progressively inward, ribs buoyant never held out. (Ragan 3.3, Bickel hissing minimum three reps.)
2. Rhythmic patterns on /s/ and /f/ at 60 bpm, engage on each pulse and release between. (Ragan 3.4.)
3. Alternating /s/-/z/ and /f/-/v/ at one phoneme per beat. Even airflow across the unvoiced to voiced switch. (Ragan 3.5.)
4. Sibilant metering 20 to 30 seconds with level flat on a meter, sternum elevated, lower ribs expanded, abs gradually inward. Then 12 second messa di voce in 4-4-4 shape with torso anchoring and thyroid tilt on the fade. (Spectrogram notes sustain protocol.)
5. Vennard lying protocol: book on abdomen rising on inhale and descending on exhale, inhale 5 hold 10 exhale 5 lengthening gradually, then quick inhalation with pursed-lip exhale as long as possible. (Vocal Athlete chapter 5.)
6. Belt rib expansion against a belt at the seventh ribs on a hiss, abdominals drawing in while intercostals hold sideward expansion. Stop before pain, build slowly. (Bickel.)
7. Card buzzing: ooh, then pucker buzz with air rushing past lips producing whirr, then 3x5 card edge at lips buzzing like a kazoo through short then longer patterns on one breath, more air for upper notes, then same pattern on ee as in me with the same support. Sequence hiss fully empty, card buzz, sing on ee, three to four rounds without stopping between. (Bickel.)
8. Counting aloud 1 to 10 through 1 to 40 plus per breath, projecting without yelling, soft palate lifted as at the start of a yawn, hands on ribs 8 to 10, minimum three breath cycles. (Bickel.)
9. Breath depletion conditioning with lip trill, tongue trill or /v/ until near end of breath, stop before visible or audible tension, no abrupt pull-in at onset. (Ragan 3.11.)
10. Flow-ball equivalents without the device: voiceless staccato at 60 per note detached not abrupt with release matching engagement, voiceless messa over the full breath cycle with controlled descent then an immediate legato phrase, flownation of 5-note patterns and octave with ball-equivalent ease. (Ragan 3.6 to 3.9, adapted where no device is owned.)

Advance from unvoiced to voiced work when 20 second /s/ runs steady three times with no rib collapse. Advance from short to longer card patterns when control holds. (Ragan 3.5, Bickel.) Do not tank up: take only the air the phrase needs, pace it with the inspiratory hold, and release the note with an open throat rather than clamping the folds with air left over. Extra air raises subglottic pressure and forces a pressurized release with pitch drop at phrase ends. (SingWise breath management.)

11. Inhale-hold-exhale counts: inhale 3 counts, suspend 3, exhale 3, adding one second per phase to a maximum without abdominal pressure, neck pressure or lightheadedness, then back down. A few rounds a day builds the inspiratory-hold muscles. (SingWise.)
12. Hiss to voiced bridge: sustained hiss then /z/-/o/-/z/-/s/ on one breath and note, four beats each, holding the inspiratory position throughout. Shows how little air voicing needs. (SingWise.)
13. Rapid counting on /z/ or /v/: 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8-7-6-5 down and back, steady stream. (SingWise.)

## 10. Onset

Balanced onset is airflow and adduction simultaneous. Silent /h/ encourages it. Voiced /b/ builds brief pressure for firmer thyroarytenoid engagement. /m/ and /n/ add resonance. /y/ as in yawn with staccato builds articulation and phonation independence for tongue tension. (Ragan onsets.)

Staccato on single pitch starting on /ba/ then silent /h/ image for vowel-initiated words, precise pitch with no scooping, equal resonance, then triad then arpeggio. Abdominal in and out at the same rate as fold adduction and abduction, release between as important as engagement, never bearing down. (Ragan 4.10.) Water-bubble staccato on arpeggio with even bubble elevation per note, ascending and descending by half steps, varied dynamics to read bubble height. (Ragan 4.11.) Staccato to legato on arpeggio with silent /h/, breath taken before legato if needed, tempo about 110 with variability. (Ragan 4.12.)

Compression drills from the spectrogram notes: 1-3-5-3-1 arpeggio on Bah Gah Kah or imaginary H onset, four crisp staccato notes plus one sustained legato note, stopping staccato by pausing exhalation rather than clamping the throat. Fry to modal slide from relaxed low creak into Mmmm-ah preserving closure and rib expansion. Silent laugh to flow siren on Hoo-ah for pressed voices. (Spectrogram notes compression protocol.)

Silent H method: start saying hah as in heart with airflow first, then make the H quieter until inaudible while still thinking it. Put it on a 12321 pattern transposed up by semitone. Never start on a glottal onset or the throat closes and the pitch scoops. Little and often. (Vocaltechnique coordinated onset.)

## 11. Resonance and placement with drones

Hum to vowel on C3 E3 G3 A3 C4, 4 seconds hum then same note on oo as in you, against a drone on each pitch. If a vowel needs more air than the straw did, it is pushed.

NEE placement on C4 to G4 on ee as in see, tongue edges against upper molars, dorsum high and forward, tip near lower teeth, 4 seconds each, two passes. Repeat one vowel while scaling size large and hollow to tiny on constant pitch, keeping vowel identity consistent. (Docx line 125.)

Twang through baby cry wae, taunt nyeah, race car neeyeow, then dialed back about 70 percent into conversational tone. Thumb under chin monitors pressure without downward bulging. (Docx line 126.) Pinched-nose drill with double-g between ng hum and ah or ee until pinched and unpinched sound identical for velopharyngeal leak. (Docx line 127.)

Vowel chain on G3 through ee eh ah oh oo, 5 seconds each with pitch and level held, posterior dorsum elevated, jaw refusing the dark lower corner. (Docx line 162.) Twang A and B on C4 to G4 on ee plain, then twang, then plain.

Lip trill arpeggio from comfortably low range ascending by half steps to top of easy range before descending, varied speeds, thumb and finger at lips toward pucker if needed. Chromatic lip trill from low range ascending by half steps with neutral larynx, finger at front of larynx or thumb and finger in thyrohyoid gap for cueing, tempo 60. Tongue trill or raspberry as alternative. (Ragan 4.8, 4.9.)

## 12. Weight thinning in order

Do weight before resonance. Resonance-first pulls folds heavy.

1. Pitch-anchor scaling. Comfortable baseline note matched through the day until found without reference. Ascending scales on soft hum or straw in head-dominant mix, not chest voice. Chest vibration rises and lightens toward hard palate. If it flips into disconnected airy falsetto, use 3.5 mm straw or soft /m/ hum. (Docx lines 103 to 107.)
2. Messa di voce weight gradients. Single vowel ee eh or ah on comfortable mid pitch, slide heavy buzzy forte down to ultra-light smooth pianissimo and back without changing pitch or vowel size. Listen for buzz dissolving into flute-like smooth tone with minimum throat effort. If the vowel darkens into a yawn, smile slightly or anchor tongue high. (Docx lines 108 to 112.)
3. Patrick Star underfull isolation. Large hollow yawn-like space at moderate to low pitch, lightest softest least buzzy weight on sentences, then shrink space back to neutral once thinness is stable. Hollow dopey round sound proves folds thin without high pitch or tension. Fry creeping in means breath flow dropped. (Docx lines 113 to 117.)
4. Heavy-heavy-light ping-ponging. One small resonance size on one pitch, alternate buzzy buzzy smooth in rhythm or glide between Overfull and Full. Contrast nerdy ducky buzz against crisp tone. Back of tongue arched near upper molars so size stays locked. (Docx lines 118 to 122.)
5. Light phrase. Two-bar repertoire phrase at the light setting, three reps. If buzz returns, one rep of item 2.

## 13. Tenor mix bridging

Duck squawk and cackle across the middle fifth to locate the pharyngeal core without neck soreness, then sustain and soften into a rounded vowel feeling notes pulled from support rather than pushed from throat. Pinpoint messa di voce on upper-bridge pitches starting pinpoint pianissimo in adducted pharyngeal falsetto, 4 second swell to forte recruiting thyroarytenoid without larynx jam or crack, 4 second fade back. Half-messa forte to piano over 8 seconds on sustained tones or descending chromatics to shed chest weight on descent. Nay-nay and baa-baa on octave slides and arpeggios for equilibrium, gug mum bub for over-brighteners with lyrics stripped from the hardest phrase first. Curbing as mild moan-like hold on mid-closed vowels to connect the first two bridges. (Docx lines 84 to 87.)

Chiaroscuro whisper for resonance tuning without fold wear, internalized low-effort turbulent airflow shaped with minimal mouth movement, tuned with affects, matching whisper pitches while keeping warm under-vowel present. (Docx lines 51 to 54.)

Whoop timbre You-hoo for flute-like release, hey timbre Hey for upper-harmonic ring, synthesized into mix rather than pure whoop too early on close vowels. (Docx line 50.) Passive vowel migration below whoop by holding tract shape stable, active modification into and within whoop by adjusting articulators. (Docx line 49.)

## 14. Articulation and prosody drills

Heat from fire slowly three times holding forward position, then three times faster without dropping back. If /h/ turns harsh or throat tightens, reset on one straw breath. She sees three green trees three times moderate, then one real sentence at same placement. Vowel chains mee mae mah moh moo keeping posterior dorsum elevated. Sibilants /s/ /t/ /d/ forward at ridge with lip corners retracted in relaxed smile. Prosody with melodic pitch excursions on emphasis while holding weight light, wider range, upward or circumflex inflections at clause boundaries instead of fry, micro-pauses for buoyant cadence. Non-verbal sounds from /h/ or /i/ posture in head-dominant mix with elevated velum. (Docx lines 130 to 164.)

## 15. Long holds and agility as readouts

Ten-second holds on six pitches with plain vowels, then the holds preset for drift, stability and vibrato presence. Messa di voce 6 seconds at G3 and C4. Drone against alone separates memory from production: drift large alone only means held pitch fades, wavering high with drone on means production. (Diagnostics reference.)

R&B agility only after weight is stable: 5-note pentatonic run at half speed staccato then half speed legato then full speed with lighter weight, tongue tip forward at lower teeth, jaw still in mirror, fingers under chin checking for tongue-root pushing. Consonant-free vowel isolation on /i/ or /u/ across the passaggio. (Spectrogram notes R&B protocol.)

Tanking up overcrowds the lungs and raises subglottic pressure, then forces a pressurized glottal release with leftover air and pitch drop at phrase ends. Practice given: inhale three counts, suspend three, exhale three, adding one second per phase to a maximum without abdominal, neck or lightheaded pressure, then back down. Unvoiced to voiced bridging drill given: sustained hiss then /z/-/o/-/z/-/s/ on one breath and note, four beats each, showing how little air voicing needs. Rapid 1-2-3-4-5-6-7-8-7-6-5 down pattern on /z/ or /v/. Use: the appoggio versus pushing distinction behind sections 2 and 9, and the tanking-up warning added to section 9 practice rules. https://www.singwise.com/articles/breath-management-support-of-the-singing-voice

Vocaltechnique coordinated onset. Three onsets: aspirate with airflow first, glottal with folds closed first, coordinated with both together giving the most speech-like connection. Method: start saying hah as in heart, then make the H quieter until inaudible while still thinking it. Then put it on a 12321 pattern transposed up by semitone. Never start beginners on glottal onset or they push from a closed throat and scoop. Onset work is little and often like gym work. Use: the silent /h/ method behind section 3 and the no-scoop target in section 10. https://vocaltechnique.co.uk/coordinated-onset/
