# Acoustic Spectrogram Decomposition and Biomechanical Vocal Pedagogy for Multimodal Singing Assistance Agents

## Multimodal Agent Architectures for 2D Spectrogram Decomposition

For centuries, vocal pedagogy relied on an apprentice-master transmission model wherein an instructor diagnosed biomechanical inefficiencies by ear and prescribed adjustments through subjective, metaphorical imagery—such as instructing a singer to "place the tone in the mask" or "spin the breath". The fundamental limitation of this traditional paradigm is that the primary physiological subsystems of phonation—the respiratory pump, the laryngeal oscillator, and the supraglottal resonator—operate largely hidden from conscious visual inspection. During independent practice, vocalists lack an objective acoustic reference to calibrate the discrepancy between internal bone-conducted self-perception and radiated acoustic output. Acoustic visualization software, exemplified by clinical and pedagogical platforms such as VoceVista Video Pro, Overtone Analyzer, and Praat, resolves this ambiguity by converting radiated sound waves into real-time Knowledge of Performance (KP) biofeedback. By externalizing vocal-tract resonance configurations and glottal source behaviors into two-dimensional geometric patterns, spectrographic displays bypass metaphorical ambiguity and mitigate cognitive efficacy biases across singers of varying skill levels.

When designing an artificial intelligence singing assistant, a critical architectural question arises: can a computational agent decompose a 2D frequency-time graph (a spectrogram) or a 2D frequency-intensity graph (a power spectrum) to diagnose vocal mechanics—such as advising a practitioner to sing "lighter," "heavier," or with "more compression"—without processing raw audio waveforms in an auditory manner? Contemporary research in computer vision and multimodal machine learning confirms that Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and hybrid audio-visual agents can extract highly granular vocal diagnostics directly from 2D spectrographic representations.

### Visual Spectrogram Classification and Patch Tokenization

A spectrogram extends a single-instant frequency spectrum into the temporal dimension by stacking consecutive Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) spectra side by side. While a power spectrum plots frequency along the horizontal axis and amplitude (in decibels) along the vertical axis for a single millisecond slice, a spectrogram maps time along the horizontal axis, frequency along the vertical axis, and represents spectral intensity through pixel brightness or color saturation. Depending on the analysis window length, spectrograms operate in one of two regimes: narrow-band filters provide high frequency resolution that isolates individual harmonic partials as distinct horizontal lines, whereas wide-band filters provide high temporal resolution that highlights vertical glottal pulse striations and broader spectral envelope peaks known as formants.

In a Vision-Language Model architecture, the agent does not "hear" the audio file; instead, a Vision Transformer (ViT) or Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) processes the rendered 2D spectrogram image as a spatial grid of pixels. The ViT divides the 2D spectrogram into fixed-size, non-overlapping square patches, flattens each 2D patch into a 1D vector, and applies a linear transformation to project these visual patch embeddings into the shared semantic embedding space of the Large Language Model (LLM) via cross-attention or lightweight adapter layers.

Empirical evaluations on the Visual Spectrogram Classification (VSC) benchmark demonstrate that frontier VLMs—including GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and Gemini 1.5 Pro—possess a nascent ability to interpret acoustic geometry directly from images. Because commercial audio-language models (ALMs) such as Pengi, SALMONN, and GAMA often utilize smaller underlying language backbones (for instance, Pengi relies on a 124-million-parameter GPT-2 model), feeding high-resolution spectrograms into massive commercial VLMs frequently yields superior semantic reasoning. When audio is resampled to 22,050 Hz and rendered via an STFT with a 2,048-sample window size, a 512-sample hop length, logarithmic frequency and amplitude axes, a high-contrast `viridis` colormap, explicit axis labels, and the removal of distracting colorbar legends, GPT-4o's classification accuracy jumps from 35% in zero-shot prompting to 70% in 10-shot in-context learning—outperforming both native audio-input models and human domain experts on visual spectrogram identification tasks.

### Overcoming Perceptual Bottlenecks via Feature-Enriched Desp-CoT

Despite the spatial pattern-recognition strengths of VLMs, relying solely on zero-shot visual inspection of a spectrogram introduces significant failure modes. Evaluations on multimodal audio reasoning benchmarks such as MMAU and MMAR reveal that perceptual errors—failures to accurately extract fine-grained acoustic relationships from the raw signal representation—account for the vast majority of model failures, far outweighing downstream logical reasoning errors. When models are evaluated on complex, "triggered" diagnostic queries requiring multi-step acoustic verification, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) accuracy drops precipitously from 67.2% to 58.9% on MMAR and from 81.4% to 72.6% on MMAU ($p < 0.01$). In a vocal pedagogy context, a pure vision model inspecting a 2D spectrogram image can easily identify macro-level geometries—such as the sinusoidal waves of vibrato, abrupt register breaks, or broadband noise in rock distortion—but may miscalculate subtle decibel differentials between individual harmonics ($H_1$ versus $H_2$) due to colormap quantization.

To eliminate perceptual hallucination, state-of-the-art singing assistance agents implement a hybrid Domain-Specific Feature-Enriched Descriptive Chain-of-Thought (Desp-CoT) architecture, drawing from frameworks established in _EmotionThinker_, _Audio-Reasoner_, _Step-Audio-R1_, and _Audio Flamingo Sound-CoT_. Rather than forcing the VLM to estimate decibel ratios solely from pixel brightness, the system operates as a two-stage pipeline encompassing multi-aspect signal extraction followed by modality-grounded reasoning distillation. In the first stage, the raw vocal recording—captured in an environment with a minimum signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of 42 dB via an omnidirectional condenser microphone and external audio interface—is simultaneously converted into a calibrated 2D narrow-band spectrogram image (with overlaid smoothed spectral envelope curves and Long-Term Average Spectrum profiles) and processed by a deterministic digital signal processing (DSP) script. The DSP module extracts exact numerical vectors for fundamental frequency ($f_0$), harmonic amplitude differentials ($H_1 - H_2$), spectral tilt ($\alpha$), Cepstral Peak Prominence Smoothed (CPPS), and Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio (HNR). Concurrently, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) and neural note-segmentation algorithms isolate individual vocalized notes with over 95% accuracy within a 30 ms tolerance window.

In the second stage, known as Modality-Grounded Reasoning Distillation (MGRD), the rendered 2D spectrogram image, few-shot visual reference exemplars of target vocal setups, and the structured dictionary of DSP metrics are concatenated into the multimodal context window. Following the four-stage _Audio-Reasoner_ protocol—which progresses systematically through planning, captioning, reasoning, and summarizing—the agent first generates an objective visual-acoustic caption describing the 2D graph's trajectory, verifies the visual geometry against the numerical DSP anchors, maps the acoustic state to laryngeal biomechanics, and outputs a targeted physical adjustment.

## Acoustic Markers of Vocal Weight, Glottal Closure, and Registration

To advise a vocalist on whether to adjust their coordination toward a "lighter" or "heavier" mechanism, the AI agent must translate 2D spectral features into laryngeal aerodynamics. In acoustic vocology, the perceived "weight" or "compression" of a voice is governed by the glottal source waveform—specifically the Open Quotient ($OQ$, the fraction of each vibratory cycle during which the vocal folds remain apart), the Closed Quotient ($CQ$), and the steepness of the trailing edge of the glottal air pulse (the maximum flow declination rate).

When the vocal folds adduct firmly and close abruptly during each vibratory cycle, the rapid cessation of airflow generates a sharp acoustic impulse rich in high-frequency harmonic partials, perceived auditorily as a "heavy," "metallic," "pressed," or "compressed" tone. Conversely, when the vocal folds vibrate with a thinner vertical contact phase or incomplete posterior glottal closure (steady leakage flow), the glottal airflow fluctuates gradually, yielding a steep drop-off in upper harmonic energy and a "light," "soft," or "breathy" quality.

### Mathematical Formulations of Spectral Tilt, H1-H2, and Breathiness Indices

The AI agent quantifies these glottal dynamics through three primary mathematical relationships extracted from the spectrum and spectrogram.

#### Low-Range Harmonic Differential ($H_1 - H_2$)

The low-range harmonic differential is calculated as the amplitude difference in decibels between the first harmonic (the fundamental frequency, $H_1 = f_0$) and the second harmonic ($H_2 = 2f_0$) in the power spectrum:

$$\Delta H_{1-2} = A(H_1)_{\text{dB}} - A(H_2)_{\text{dB}}$$

Because $H_1$ amplitude is directly proportional to the volume velocity of the glottal pulse during the open phase, while $H_2$ and higher harmonics depend on the skewness and abruptness of glottal closure, $H_1 - H_2$ exhibits a tight positive correlation with the Open Quotient ($OQ$). A high positive $H_1 - H_2$ value, where the bottom line on the spectrogram is intensely bright and the second line above it is comparatively dim, indicates a high $OQ$, lax vocal fold tension, and breathy or unreinforced falsetto phonation. Conversely, a low, zero, or negative $H_1 - H_2$ value, where the second harmonic is brighter than the fundamental, indicates a high $CQ$, thick vocal fold mass, and heavy or pressed glottal adduction.

#### Global Spectral Tilt ($\alpha$)

Spectral tilt measures the rate at which harmonic energy decays across the entire frequency spectrum from low to high partials, typically expressed in decibels per octave. In parametric voice synthesis and analysis models, spectral tilt scales the default power spectrum $S(\omega)$ as a function of angular frequency $\omega$ governed by the tilt parameter $\alpha \in [0, 1]$:

$$S_{\text{tilted}}(\omega) = S(\omega) \cdot \omega^{-\alpha}$$

When $\alpha \to 0$, $S(\omega)$ remains unattenuated across upper frequencies, producing a flat spectral envelope with bright, dense horizontal harmonic bands extending above 4,000 Hz on the 2D spectrogram—the hallmark of high vocal intensity, cricoid tilt, or pressed phonation. A normative speaking baseline sits near $\alpha \approx 0.35$, whereas $\alpha \to 1$ represents extreme spectral roll-off characteristic of a quiet, breathy, or whispering voice where upper harmonics vanish entirely into dark background space on the spectrogram.

#### Multiparametric Glottal Closure and Breathiness (ABI & AVQI)

Because single spectral measures can be confounded by vowel formant tuning—for instance, when the first formant $F_1$ sits directly atop $H_1$ or $H_2$ and artificially boosts its radiated amplitude—an advanced agent incorporates multiparametric indices such as the Acoustic Voice Quality Index (AVQI, where a score above the normative cut-off of $2.35$ flags dysphonic irregularity) and the Acoustic Breathiness Index (ABI, scaled from $0$ to $10$). The agent computes ABI from the sustained vowel and continuous singing segments via the exact linear combination of nine spectral, cepstral, and perturbation parameters:

$$\text{ABI} = \left( 5.0447730915 - 0.172(\text{CPPS}) - 0.193(\text{Jit}) - 1.283(\text{GNE}_{\max\text{-4500 Hz}}) - 0.396(\text{Hfno}_{\text{6000 Hz}}) + 0.01(\text{HNR-D}) + 0.017(H_1 - H_2) + 1.473(\text{Shim}_{\text{dB}}) - 0.088(\text{Shim}) - 68.295(\text{PSD}) \right) \times 2.9257400394$$

Within this formulation, CPPS (Cepstral Peak Prominence Smoothed, with a healthy sustained-vowel normative threshold of $\ge 14.47\text{ dB}$) measures the regularity and prominence of the harmonic series above background noise; $\text{GNE}_{\max\text{-4500 Hz}}$ (Glottal-to-Noise Excitation) quantifies the degree of true vocal fold oscillation versus turbulent air leakage below 4,500 Hz; $\text{Hfno}_{\text{6000 Hz}}$ measures the high-frequency noise energy ratio up to 6,000 Hz; $\text{HNR-D}$ evaluates the harmonics-to-noise ratio; Jit (Jitter) and PSD (Period Standard Deviation in milliseconds) capture short-term frequency perturbation; and Shim alongside $\text{Shim}_{\text{dB}}$ capture cycle-to-cycle amplitude perturbation.

## Grounding Physiology: Laryngeal Mechanisms, Nonlinear Coupling, and Physical Maps

For an AI agent to guide a human singer effectively, its diagnostic outputs cannot remain in the abstract realm of Fourier mathematics; they must map onto concrete anatomical structures and reproducible proprioceptive sensations ("physical maps"). Modern voice science synthesizes this mapping through three complementary frameworks: the Four Laryngeal Vibratory Mechanisms ($M_0–M_3$) codified by Roubeau, Henrich, Castellengo, and Titze; the Nonlinear Source-Filter Coupling Theory developed by Titze; and the Isolated Structural Figures of the Estill Voice Model.

### The Four Laryngeal Vibratory Mechanisms (M0–M3)

Rather than relying on ambiguous traditional labels like "chest voice," "middle voice," or "head voice"—which conflate laryngeal muscle actions with sympathetic vibrations felt in the bones of the sternum or skull—vocology classifies registration into four distinct mechanical regimes defined by the biomechanical configuration of the vocal folds. At the lowest pitch extreme lies Mechanism 0 ($M_0$, also known as pulse register, vocal fry, or _Strohbass_), in which the vocal folds are short, slack, and thick, with both the thyroarytenoid (TA) muscular body and the mucosal cover relaxed. During $M_0$ phonation, subglottal pressure and airflow remain minimal, producing slow, discrete, syncopated glottal pulses or alternating cycle vibrations at one-half or one-third of the perceived fundamental frequency.

Ascending into the primary speaking and low-to-mid singing range engages Mechanism 1 ($M_1$, corresponding to modal, heavy, chest, or chest-mix voice), wherein the entire anatomical mass of the vocal fold—including both the deep muscular vocalis/thyroarytenoid body and the superficial lamina propria/epithelium cover—participates in oscillation. As Titze notes, the bottom margin of the vocal fold is strongly adducted in modal register compared to falsetto, creating a thick vertical contact phase and a complex mucosal wave characterized by a high Closed Quotient ($CQ > 50\%$). Pedagogical categories of "chest voice" and "chest-dominant mix" reside biomechanically within $M_1$.

As pitch ascends further, the cricothyroid (CT) muscle contracts with increasing force, tilting the thyroid cartilage forward and downward relative to the cricoid cartilage to elongate and thin the vocal folds. At a critical biomechanical threshold along this continuum, the voice transitions into Mechanism 2 ($M_2$, encompassing loft, falsetto, and head voice), where the deep TA muscle body ceases large-amplitude oscillation and only the vocal ligament and thin mucosal edges vibrate. High-speed videoendoscopy indicates that $M_2$ maintains a median Closed Quotient of approximately $47.6\%$. Both breathy "falsetto" and resonant classical or contemporary "head voice" and "reinforced falsetto" operate within $M_2$; they differ not in their fundamental laryngeal mechanism, but in their degree of glottal closure and supraglottal acoustic reinforcement.

At the upper extreme of the human vocal ambitus lies Mechanism 3 ($M_3$, termed whistle register, flageolet, flute register, or _Pfeifstimme_)—with transitions documented between D#5 and D6 by Garnier et al., around C6 ($1,047\text{ Hz}$) by general clinical consensus, and strictly at or above F6–G6 ($1,397–1,568\text{ Hz}$) by Titze. While older literature debated whether $M_3$ behaves as a rigid aerodynamic orifice whistle—generating sound purely through vortex shedding at the upper edges of non-vibrating folds—versus passive self-sustaining myoelastic-aerodynamic (MEAD) oscillation, recent laryngeal high-speed videoendoscopy confirms that high-pitched operatic soprano phonation up to G6 utilizes the MEAD mechanism with full-length or shortened ("damped") glottal vibration closely resembling $M_2$ ($CQ \approx 47.6\%$), whereas true aerodynamic vortex whistling occurs as a distinct aerodynamic variant.

### Nonlinear Source-Filter Coupling and Epilaryngeal Inertance

A pivotal breakthrough for vocal coaching agents is resolving the fallacy of the linear Source-Filter model. Classical acoustic theory treated the vocal folds (Source) and the vocal tract (Filter) as independent systems, assuming that the pharyngeal and oral cavities merely filtered harmonics after they left the glottis. Titze's nonlinear source-filter coupling theory demonstrates that the vocal tract actively reflects acoustic energy back down to the vocal folds during the open phase of each glottal cycle.

When a vocalist narrows the epilarynx tube—the short supraglottal collar located immediately above the true and false vocal folds, bounded by the aryepiglottic sphincter (AES)—the acoustic impedance of the vocal tract becomes strongly inertive, exhibiting positive inertive reactance. Positive inertive reactance creates a time-delayed supraglottal pressure wave that pulls the vocal folds apart as they open and helps draw them back together as they close, working in synergy with the constraining elastic properties of the fold tissue and driving subglottal pressure. This nonlinear coupling achieves three simultaneous biomechanical advantages that directly inform agent diagnostics. First, it lowers the Phonation Threshold Pressure (PTP), allowing the vocal folds to vibrate with massive acoustic power at a fraction of the muscular and respiratory effort. Second, it skews the glottal flow waveform to the right—increasing the maximum flow declination rate—which automatically generates rich high-frequency overtones in the $2.5–3.5\text{ kHz}$ "Singer's Formant" cluster without requiring heavy, damaging TA muscle squeezing at the glottis. Third, it stabilizes register transitions; conversely, when a singer fails to maintain inertive reactance and allows a lower harmonic ($H_1$ or $H_2$) to pass directly through the first formant ($F_1$) into a zone of negative, compliant reactance, the reflected acoustic pressure opposes the vocal fold motion and triggers immediate oscillatory destabilization—manifesting on the 2D spectrogram as an involuntary voice crack or pitch jump.

## Comprehensive Technique Compendium and Diagnostic Exercise Protocols

To serve as an exhaustive operational database for a singing assistance agent, the following sections decompose seven foundational and stylistic vocal domains: Vibrato, Compression, Reinforced Falsetto, Flageolet ($M_3$), Sustain, Rock Styles, and R&B Styles. Each domain integrates biomechanical grounding, 2D spectrogram decomposition rules, physical sensation maps, and progressive exercise protocols.

### Vibrato: Neuromuscular Oscillatory Reflex and Sinusoidal Stability

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

Vibrato is a periodic, rhythmic modulation of the fundamental frequency ($f_0$) and intensity of a sustained tone, imparting acoustic warmth and carrying power while allowing the intrinsic laryngeal muscles periodic micro-recovery during high-intensity sustain. Physiologically, healthy vibrato is generated by a central nervous system reflex loop oscillating between the cricothyroid (CT) and thyroarytenoid (TA) muscles at a rate of $5.0$ to $7.0\text{ Hz}$ (cycles per second), with a pitch extent (undulation width) of $\pm 0.5$ to $\pm 1.0$ semitones ($\pm 50$ to $100$ cents) around the target tempered pitch.

For this neuromuscular tremor to emerge freely, two physical conditions must be met: subglottal air pressure ($p_{\text{sub}}$) must remain continuous and non-rigid, and the extrinsic laryngeal suspensory muscles—particularly the suprahyoid tongue-root muscles and jaw—must remain free of static clamping. Pulling the tongue tip up and back or depressing the tongue root into the pharynx locks the hyoid bone and thyroid cartilage, either extinguishing vibrato into a rigid straight tone or forcing a compensatory, slow epigastric or jaw wobble.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

On a narrow-band 2D spectrogram (and within dedicated modules like VoceVista's Vibrato View), vibrato manifests as synchronized sinusoidal waves across all horizontal harmonic lines. Because harmonics are integer multiples of the fundamental ($f_n = n \cdot f_0$), the vertical visual excursion (wave height in Hz) scales linearly with harmonic number: the fifth harmonic ($H_5$) exhibits five times the vertical pixel oscillation in Hz as $H_1$, making upper harmonics the optimal visual target for agent tracking. Furthermore, as the oscillating harmonics sweep repeatedly across stationary vocal-tract formants ($F_1–F_4$), they produce periodic brightness modulations (amplitude vibrato or shimmer modulation) that alternate in or out of phase depending on whether the harmonic sits on the upper or lower slope of a formant peak.

When diagnosing vibrato pathologies on the 2D graph, the agent distinguishes three primary deviations from the healthy $5.0–7.0\text{ Hz}$ sinusoidal baseline. A wobble fault appears as a sluggish wave rate below $4.5\text{ Hz}$ with excessive pitch excursion exceeding $\pm 1.5$ semitones, indicating slack glottal adduction, heavy TA over-weighting, or weak torso anchoring. Conversely, a tremolo or bleat fault manifests as a rapid, jagged wave rate above $7.5\text{ Hz}$ with narrow pitch extent, signaling hyper-adducted glottal squeezing, high-larynx constriction, or epigastric locking. Finally, a straight-tone or delayed-onset pattern appears as flat horizontal harmonic lines with subtle micro-jitter that transition smoothly into terminal sinusoidal waves—a coordination frequently employed intentionally as an expressive device in R&B and contemporary pop styling.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To calibrate vibrato through diaphragmatic pulsing and laryngeal release, the vocalist begins by placing the tongue tip flat behind the lower front teeth and executing a silent, dog-like pant to feel natural diaphragmatic bounce. The singer then vocalizes a light $M_1$ or $M_2$ "Huh-huh-huh-haaaah" on a comfortable mid-range pitch, allowing the initial rhythmic airflow pulses to merge into a continuous tone while maintaining a slight thyroid cartilage tilt akin to a puppy whimper. To awaken the CT-TA pitch-changing reflex directly without jaw oscillation, the singer transitions to a semitone trill acceleration drill, placing a pencil horizontally across the premolars or resting two fingers gently on the chin to monitor jaw stillness. By alternating between two adjacent semitones (such as G4 and Ab4) on an /u/ or /i/ vowel—starting at quarter-note speed, accelerating through eighth notes and triplets into a sixteenth-note blur, and then releasing into a single sustained pitch—the vocalist encourages the intrinsic laryngeal oscillation to continue autonomously.

### Compression: Glottal Adduction, Flow Phonation, and Onset Calibration

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

In contemporary vocal pedagogy, "compression" refers to the medial adduction resistance of the true vocal folds against subglottal airflow ($p_{\text{sub}}$), governed by the lateral cricoarytenoid, interarytenoid, and thyroarytenoid muscles. The central pedagogical objective is achieving Flow Phonation—the biomechanical sweet spot along the glottal continuum (progressing from Breathy to Flow, Neutral, and Pressed) where maximum acoustic intensity and harmonic richness are generated with minimal subglottal pressure and zero false vocal fold interference.

A critical error among developing singers is conflating true glottal compression with supraglottal constriction, which involves squeezing the false vocal folds together as in a heavy grunt. True flow compression requires a dual action: firm, clean approximation of the vocal processes and membranous vocal folds, coupled simultaneously with wide lateral retraction of the false vocal folds (felt kinesthetically as a silent laugh or open pharyngeal space) and stable torso anchoring.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

The singing assistance agent evaluates compression across two primary regions of the 2D spectrogram: the Note Onset Boundary at the left vertical edge of the phonation event and the Mid-Note Harmonic Envelope. During a breathy or under-compressed onset, the spectrogram displays a vertical cloud of diffuse, aperiodic high-frequency noise before the horizontal harmonic lines appear, caused by aspirate air escaping when subglottal pressure ($p_{\text{sub}}$) rises prior to glottal closure; this is followed by a weak mid-note spectrum where $H_1 - H_2 > +5\text{ dB}$ and $\text{CPPS} < 14.47\text{ dB}$. In contrast, a hard or pressed glottal attack produces a sharp, vertical full-spectrum line (a broadband click striation across all frequencies) preceding the harmonics because the vocal folds slam shut and accumulate excessive $p_{\text{sub}}$ before blowing apart, resulting in a suppressed fundamental where $H_1 - H_2 < -2\text{ dB}$. When the vocalist achieves a balanced, simultaneous staccato or flow onset, the horizontal harmonics ignite instantaneously at full brightness from $H_1$ through $H_8$ with no preceding noise cloud and no vertical click spike, maintaining $H_1$ and $H_2$ within $\pm 2\text{ dB}$ of one another and elevating $\text{CPPS}$ above $15\text{ dB}$.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To train balanced glottal compression, the vocalist utilizes staccato breath-cessation drills. As established in classical and contemporary pedagogy, proper staccato is executed by coordinating a simultaneous start of glottal adduction and $p_{\text{sub}}$ rise, and stopping the tone cleanly by ceasing exhalation—halting the abdominal and ribcage pump—rather than slamming the glottis shut at the offset. The vocalist sings a 1–3–5–3–1 arpeggio on plosive syllables (_Bah, Gah, Kah_) or a pure vowel /a/ using four crisp staccato notes followed by one sustained legato note, maintaining the exact silent-laugh false fold retraction between notes. For singers exhibiting chronic air leak (elevated ABI), the agent prescribes a bottom-up vocal fry-to-modal slide, wherein the practitioner initiates a relaxed, low-effort $M_0$ creak at effort level 2 on the Stevens scale, feeling the gentle popping of the vocal fold edges without neck tension, and smoothly slides upward into an $M_1$ sustained vowel on "Mmmm-ah" while preserving cord closure and ribcage expansion.

### Reinforced Falsetto: Inertive M2 Augmentation and Aryepiglottic Twang

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

When male vocalists—or female vocalists utilizing a light mechanism—ascend above their primary passaggio into Mechanism 2 ($M_2$), the cricothyroid (CT) muscle stretches the vocal folds so that only the thin ligamentous edge vibrates. Left unadjusted, $M_2$ often defaults to a slack, open posterior glottis ("flute" or aspirate falsetto), which sounds hollow, airy, and incapable of cutting through dense musical instrumentation. Reinforced Falsetto transforms this thin $M_2$ source into a piercing, resonant, full-bodied tone that mimics the power of $M_1$ chest-mix without adding heavy thyroarytenoid mass.

Biomechanically, reinforced falsetto is constructed via a four-part structural coordination. First, CT dominance maintains a thin $M_2$ vocal fold edge by keeping the thyroid cartilage fully tilted in a cry or sob posture. Second, the interarytenoid and lateral cricoarytenoid muscles adduct to achieve complete membranous and arytenoid closure, sealing the posterior glottal chink so zero unphonated air escapes and creating stiff, adducted thin folds rather than slack folds. Third, the vocalist narrows the aryepiglottic sphincter (AES) to induce "twang," constricting the epilarynx tube to generate strong positive inertive reactance that reflects acoustic energy back to the thin folds, skews the glottal flow pulse, and ignites the $2.5–4.0\text{ kHz}$ harmonic cluster. Fourth, because narrowing the AES occurs in close anatomical proximity to the ventricular folds, the singer must actively retract the false vocal folds via a silent laugh sensation and engage torso anchoring so the bright twang does not collapse into a strangled throat squeeze.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

When a singer transitions from slack falsetto into reinforced falsetto on a single sustained pitch, the 2D spectrogram undergoes a dramatic visual transformation. In slack falsetto, only $H_1$ and a faint $H_2$ are visible on the screen, while the upper frequency region between $1.5\text{ kHz}$ and $5.0\text{ kHz}$ remains either completely black (empty of harmonic energy) or filled with aperiodic breathiness haze accompanied by $H_1 - H_2 > +10\text{ dB}$. Once reinforced falsetto is engaged, the inter-harmonic noise vanishes as $\text{HNR-D}$ and $\text{CPPS}$ surge upward, $H_2$ and $H_3$ brighten to match or exceed the intensity of $H_1$, and a brilliant, concentrated band of harmonic energy illuminates the $2,800–3,800\text{ Hz}$ zone (the AES twang formant cluster), even though the fundamental frequency remains cleanly in $M_2$ without a lower-octave chest break.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To establish reinforced falsetto, the vocalist practices a witch's cackle or duck quack transitioning into a sob release. In the upper $M_2$ range (such as E4–C5 for tenors and baritones), the singer initiates a hyper-nasal, brassy "Nyah-nyah-nyah" or witch's cackle on an /æ/ or /e/ vowel to lock in maximum AES narrowing and complete glottal closure. Once the bright high-frequency ring is verified on the spectrogram, the singer holds the sustained note while slightly lowering the larynx—adding a mild yawn or Estill "Sob" quality—and lifting the soft palate, which warms the timbre while preserving the $3\text{ kHz}$ spectral ring. As a secondary progression, the vocalist performs an $M_2$ _messa di voce_ siren crescendo: starting on a soft, clean $M_2$ "Hoo" (/u/ vowel) at C5, the singer gradually morphs the vowel toward /o/ or /e/ while progressively engaging torso anchoring (ribcage expansion) and AES twang to swell the radiated volume by +15 dB without flipping down into $M_1$.

### Flageolet and Whistle Register: M3 Damping, MEAD Dynamics, and Subordinate H2

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

At the extreme zenith of the human vocal range—typically above C6 ($1,047\text{ Hz}$) for sopranos and high above the standard $M_2$ ceiling for male voices—lies Mechanism 3 ($M_3$), historically termed the Flageolet (named by Mozart in reference to high flute-like tones or string harmonics) or Whistle Register. While academic literature often uses _flageolet_ and _whistle_ interchangeably, advanced contemporary and classical pedagogy draws a vital functional distinction between two coordination styles within this high pitch regime.

The first coordination is Connected Flageolet (damped high $M_2/M_3$), utilized by operatic coloratura sopranos—such as Diana Damrau performing the _Queen of the Night_ aria up to F6—and crossover virtuosos such as Dimash Qudaibergen navigating seamless multi-octave slides. As corroborated by high-speed videoendoscopy of operatic sopranos singing from C6 to G6 ($1,568\text{ Hz}$), this coordination relies on the standard myoelastic-aerodynamic (MEAD) self-sustaining oscillation principle ($CQ \approx 47.6\%$) combined with extreme longitudinal CT tension and posterior or anterior glottal damping, which shortens the vibrating glottal chink much like a violinist pressing a finger high up a string. Crucially, the vocalist couples the fundamental frequency ($f_0$) directly to the first formant ($F_1 \approx f_0$) using a narrow, high-pharyngeal setup so the tone remains acoustically connected to the middle register and projects unamplified across a concert hall. The second coordination is Disconnected Aerodynamic Whistle, frequently utilized in pop and R&B ornamentations such as Mariah Carey's sustained G6 passages. This variant is characterized by an ultra-thin, minimally vibrating glottal slit where sound generation is dominated or assisted by aerodynamic vortex shedding at the upper vocal fold margins, accompanied by significantly reduced mean airflow rates in most subjects. It produces a delicate, flute-like tone that requires close microphone amplification and often exhibits a slight perceptual gap or breathy reset when transitioning from a lower belted phrase.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

Walker's acoustic and aerodynamic analyses alongside modern spectrographic studies establish two definitive 2D visual signatures that allow an AI agent to distinguish $M_3$ flageolet and whistle phonations from high $M_2$ head voice. First, the spectrogram displays an exceptionally sparse harmonic count: because $f_0$ sits between $1,000\text{ Hz}$ and $2,000\text{ Hz}$, very few integer harmonics fall within the human auditory bandwidth, leaving only 2 to 4 widely spaced horizontal lines visible on the graph. Second, the spectrum exhibits an asymmetrical envelope with a subordinate second harmonic ($H_2$): whereas high $M_2$ head voice maintains a symmetrical, graded spectral envelope where $H_1$ and $H_2$ are both prominent, $M_3$ whistle and flageolet phonations are frequently characterized by an asymmetrical form in which $H_1$ is intensely bright, $H_2$ at $2f_0$ is conspicuously weak, suppressed, or missing, and $H_3$ at $3f_0$ re-emerges.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To access connected flageolet without squeezing the throat, the singer executes a closed-mouth pharyngeal siren ("puppy squeak"), keeping the lips gently closed or forming a tiny pin-hole /u/ aperture, retracting the false vocal folds via a silent laugh, and initiating a high, quiet puppy whimper with extreme thyroid tilt in upper $M_2$. As the pitch slides upward past B5–C6, the singer imagines the sound narrowing into a tiny laser beam behind the upper molars and soft palate while deliberately reducing airflow volume—allowing the vocal folds to damp naturally into $M_3$ rather than blowing them apart with excess subglottal air. Conversely, to discover the aerodynamic pop whistle aperture, singers take advantage of the fact that glottal whistles can be generated via both ingressive (inhaled) and egressive (exhaled) airflow. By gently inhaling a tiny, high-pitched squeak (ingressive phonation) at minimal volume to feel the exact anterior glottal chink, the vocalist can immediately reverse the air stream into an exhaled whistle on the exact same laryngeal posture while keeping the jaw relaxed.

### Sustain: Appoggio, Transdiaphragmatic Pressure, and Messa di Voce

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

Sustaining long, high-intensity musical phrases without pitch sag, timbral collapse, or muscular fatigue requires mastering the coordination of the respiratory subsystem—historically termed _appoggio_ and modernized in vocology as breath management or breath coordination. Lung volume directly governs elastic recoil pressures, which in turn dictate subglottal pressure ($p_{\text{sub}}$), Sound Pressure Level (SPL), and spectral tilt.

At the beginning of a full inhalation, the passive elastic recoil of the ribcage and lungs exceeds the target $p_{\text{sub}}$ required for balanced phonation; if unchecked, this excess air blasts through the glottis, forcing the vocal folds to hyper-adduct (press) to hold back the air. Conversely, at the end of a long sustain as lung volume drops below functional residual capacity, passive recoil vanishes, and the singer risks squeezing the extrinsic neck muscles to maintain volume. True sustain relies on maintaining a comfortably high sternum, executing a silent non-clavicular inhalation followed by a brief respiratory suspension, and balancing dynamic antagonism between the inspiratory intercostal/diaphragmatic muscles (tracking transdiaphragmatic pressure) and the expiratory abdominal wall (Torso Anchoring), coupled with epilaryngeal inertance so that glottal airflow consumption remains exceptionally low.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

The agent evaluates sustain over multi-second time windows using the Long-Term Average Spectrum (LTAS), the Audio Waveform Envelope, and the Fundamental Pitch Trajectory ($f_0$). When a vocalist suffers from end-of-phrase collapse during the final 25% of a sustained note, the horizontal harmonics sag downward by 15 to 40 cents (flat intonation), vibrato waves become erratic or flatten out, and upper harmonics above $3\text{ kHz}$ fade into darkness (increasing spectral tilt $\alpha$) as subglottal pressure drops. If the singer exhibits an over-blown initiation, an initial burst of excessive amplitude and suppressed $H_1$ appears in the first 500 ms followed by a sudden drop in harmonic density, indicating a failure to suspend and meter the initial elastic breath recoil. In a mastered _messa di voce_ (a sustained crescendo-decrescendo from $p \to f \to p$), $f_0$ remains locked within $\pm 5$ cents of the tempered target ruler; as intensity rises during the crescendo, upper harmonics ($H_3–H_{12}$) bloom progressively brighter without noise contamination, and during the decrescendo, the $2.8–3.2\text{ kHz}$ formant ring remains visible even as overall amplitude tapers.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To train respiratory suspension and airflow metering, the vocalist begins lying flat on the back—or standing with hands clasped behind the head as a posture corrector—to eliminate upper-chest clavicular heaving and feel lateral rib and lower abdominal expansion ("breathing low down into the hips" or "sniffing a rose"). After a silent inhalation and a 1-second suspension, the singer exhales on a steady, high-pressure sibilant ("Ssssss" or "Zzzzzz") for 20 to 30 seconds, keeping the sternum elevated and lower ribs expanded while the lower abdominal wall gradually draws inward, monitoring a smartphone SPL meter or waveform envelope to keep decibel output perfectly flat. Subsequently, the singer executes a spectrogram-guided _messa di voce_ on a single mid-range pitch (/a/ or /i/) over 12 seconds (4 seconds soft-to-loud, 4 seconds peak sustain, and 4 seconds loud-to-soft), watching the real-time 2D spectrogram and engaging Torso Anchoring and Thyroid Tilt during the decrescendo phase so the voice thins smoothly from $M_1$ into inertive $M_2$ without cracking or going flat.

### Rock Styles: High-Intensity Belting, Subharmonics, and Ventricular Nonlinearities

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

Rock, metal, blues, and alternative vocal styles deliberately exploit acoustic regimes that classical pedagogy historically rejected: ultra-high-intensity modal projection (Belting) and intentional Nonlinear Phenomena (NLP)—including vocal distortion, grit, rattle, growls, and screams. From an evolutionary and psychoacoustic perspective, vocal distortion emulates biological nonlinearities that signal high arousal, resist auditory habituation, and command immediate listener attention. The pedagogical challenge for a singing assistant is enabling the vocalist to generate ferocious timbral distortion while preserving complete structural homeostasis of the delicate true vocal fold mucosa.

In nonlinear dynamics theory, normal clean phonation represents a stable limit cycle attractor driven by synchronized fundamental eigenmodes (modes 10 and 11) of the true vocal folds. Rock distortion shifts the vocal apparatus into higher-order dynamical regimes by recruiting supraglottal oscillators—specifically the ventricular folds (false vocal folds, FVF) and the aryepiglottic complex (cuneiform/corniculate cartilages and aryepiglottic folds)—to co-oscillate above the true vocal folds as a coupled oscillator system. When the false vocal folds adduct partially into a narrow mid-position above vibrating true vocal folds, aerodynamic coupling locks the ventricular folds into a $1:2$ or $1:3$ entrainment ratio with the true vocal folds (or excites higher-order true fold eigenmodes 20, 21, and 30), producing period-doubling or period-tripling subharmonics that add a warm, gravelly undertone an octave ($f_0/2$) or twelfth ($f_0/3$) below the sung melody. When two structures such as the true vocal folds and aryepiglottic folds oscillate simultaneously at independent, non-integer-related frequencies ($f_1$ and $f_2$), they produce biphonation (harmonic sidebands and rattle). Under higher airflow and tighter supraglottal adduction, coupling ratios between the aryepiglottic complex/ventricular folds and true folds reach $1:4$ to $1:7$ or transition into deterministic chaos (a strange attractor), generating the irregular, aperiodic wall of rough noise characteristic of heavy metal screams and growls. For clean or gritty high-range rock notes, the singer relies on Estill's Belt recipe: Cricoid Cartilage Tilt (shouting posture), Thick True Vocal Folds ($M_1$), High Larynx, Narrow AES (Twang), Maximal Head/Neck and Torso Anchoring, and—whenever clean power is desired—Retracted False Vocal Folds to prevent vocal fold trauma.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

The 2D narrow-band spectrogram provides an unmistakable visual fingerprint for every category of rock vocal production. A clean high belt displays zero subharmonics or inter-harmonic noise; $H_1$ remains moderate while $H_2$ through $H_{10}$ form thick, blazing horizontal bars with a flat spectral tilt ($\alpha \to 0$) and an elevated $F_1/F_2$ vowel placement achieved by opening the front of the mouth rather than lowering the back of the jaw. When subharmonic grit is engaged, razor-sharp, parallel horizontal lines appear midway ($0.5 f_0, 1.5 f_0, 2.5 f_0$) or at one-third intervals ($0.33 f_0, 0.67 f_0$) between every integer harmonic of the sung pitch, whereas biphonation sidebands appear as cluster bands of closely spaced satellite lines flanking each main harmonic. Crucially, the agent distinguishes healthy supraglottal chaotic distortion from pathological true-fold strain by checking the harmonic skeleton: in healthy supraglottal distortion, the underlying true vocal fold harmonics ($f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0$) remain visible as bright horizontal rails embedded within the vertical striations of low-frequency modulation, and the singer can instantly switch back to a clean tone with $\text{CPPS} \ge 14.47\text{ dB}$. If the true harmonics vanish into high-frequency aspirate hiss and subsequent clean test vowels show elevated Jitter, Shimmer, and $\text{AVQI} > 2.35$, the singer is grinding the true vocal folds rather than engaging the false folds—triggering an immediate agent safety stop.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To build the foundation for high rock belting without throat constriction, the vocalist performs a taxi-shout to twang-belt calibration: placing the hands behind the head to engage torso anchoring and keeping the tongue flat against the lower teeth, the singer calls out an enthusiastic "Hey! Taxi!" or "Yeah!" on a mid-high pitch to activate Cricoid Tilt and thick $M_1$ folds. The singer then sustains the "Yeah" (/æ/ or /ɛ/ vowel with the front of the mouth open), adds AES twang, and checks the spectrogram to verify bright upper harmonics with zero inter-harmonic fuzz. To layer safe rock distortion over a belt or reinforced falsetto, the singer uses a clean-to-grit ventricular toggle: first establishing a bright, twanged note in $M_1$ or $M_2$ so that narrow AES inertive reactance shields the true folds, the singer maintains strong abdominal support and imagines fogging up a cold window or making an exasperated "Ugh!" sound high in the throat. This action brings the false vocal folds from wide retraction slightly inward until the $1:2$ or $1:3$ subharmonic rattle ignites, after which the singer practices toggling cleanly from _Clean to Grit to Clean_ on a single breath to verify voluntary muscular separation.

### R&B Styles: High-Velocity Melisma, Laryngeal Decoupling, and Micro-Agility

#### Biomechanical Grounding & Physical Map

Contemporary R&B, soul, gospel, and pop vocalism—exemplified by the intricate, "hummingbird-like" runs and ornamentations of artists like Tori Kelly—demands extreme melismatic agility: the capacity to execute rapid, multi-note pitch cascades (pentatonic, blues, or diatonic scales) at high velocity with pinpoint intonation accuracy.

From a biomechanical standpoint, high-speed melisma is governed by the law of mechanical inertia. If a singer attempts to execute a sixteenth-note run at 140 BPM while carrying heavy $M_1$ vocal weight (thick TA body engagement) or while moving the jaw, tongue root, and extrinsic neck muscles on every pitch change, the massive tissue inertia blurs the pitch transitions into a sluggish glissando. Agile melisma requires four coordinated physical adjustments. First, the vocalist must achieve laryngeal decoupling (jaw and tongue independence), keeping the jaw stabilized and resting the tongue flat and forward against the lower front teeth so that pitch changes are executed exclusively by rapid, micro-elastic adjustments of the intrinsic cricothyroid (CT) and thyroarytenoid (TA) muscles. Second, as the run ascends or accelerates, the vocalist must execute calibrated vocal weight reduction by thinning the true vocal fold body-cover (tilting the thyroid cartilage slightly toward a light $M_1/M_2$ mix) to reduce oscillating mass and allow instantaneous pitch locking. Third, rather than separating notes with clumsy jaw bounces or excessive subglottal air bursts ("H-H-H") that deplete breath, elite R&B agility relies on a continuous, pressurized air column with subtle micro-oscillations in glottal adduction or ultra-light diaphragmatic bounces that articulate each note boundary within a 30 ms transition window. Fourth, R&B phrases frequently integrate stylistic onset and offset ornamentations, combining aspirate or glottal scoop onsets, straight-tone sustains that bloom into late terminal vibrato, and rapid cry or flip offsets into $M_2$.

#### 2D Spectrogram Decomposition Rules

On a 2D spectrogram, the transition between two sung notes is preserved as a continuous geometric trajectory. The AI agent evaluates R&B melisma by zooming into the horizontal time axis (using a shorter STFT window for high temporal resolution) and running automated note segmentation, which achieves over 95% accuracy against expert annotations at a 30 ms tolerance window. In a well-executed R&B run, the fundamental and its upper harmonics trace a crisp step-function geometry—flat, stable horizontal plateaus for each target pitch connected by rapid, vertical step transitions lasting less than 30 ms, with continuous formant brightness across the entire cascade. If the singer exhibits a smearing or slurring fault, the harmonics trace a diagonal, wavy ramp rather than discrete horizontal steps and never stabilize on the tempered note rulers, indicating excessive vocal weight (thick $M_1$ mass) or extrinsic tongue and jaw dragging. Conversely, if dark vertical gaps and high-frequency breathy noise bursts appear between every step of the run (an aspirate dropout fault), the spectrogram reveals that the singer is re-initiating an aspirate onset ("Ha-ha-ha") on every note rather than maintaining continuous glottal flow.

#### Progressive Exercise Protocols

To program accurate neuromuscular pitch targets into memory, the vocalist practices a half-time staccato-to-legato subdivision drill on a 5-note pentatonic run (such as C5–A4–G4–E4–C4) while resting the tongue tip forward against the lower front teeth and monitoring jaw stillness in a mirror. The singer first articulates the pattern at 50% tempo using light staccato pulses on "Eh" or "Ah," stopping each note via exhalation control rather than throat clamping, then connects the notes into a slow, deliberate legato at 50% tempo while watching the VoceVista note sliders to verify flat horizontal steps, and finally doubles the tempo while consciously thinning the vocal fold mass by 30% via thyroid tilt so the notes bounce effortlessly. To eliminate compensatory jaw or tongue movement, the vocalist pairs this with a consonant-free vowel isolation run, placing the index and middle fingers lightly under the chin (on the soft suprahyoid muscles behind the mandible) to detect any downward tongue-root pushing while executing rapid 3-note and 5-note neighbor-tone turns on a single narrow vowel (/i/ or /u/) across the $M_1/M_2$ passaggio.

## System Integration and Agent Prompting Protocols

Deploying this vocological architecture within an interactive singing assistance agent requires careful calibration of how and when visual-acoustic feedback is delivered to the human learner. Empirical motor-learning research in vocal pedagogy distinguishes between concurrent feedback (real-time visual displays presented during active phonation) and terminal feedback (post-phonation spectral analysis and agent commentary delivered after the vocal phrase concludes). While concurrent real-time spectrograms and pitch-trajectory displays provide immediate Knowledge of Performance (KP) that helps beginners discover unfamiliar laryngeal postures—such as observing the $3\text{ kHz}$ band light up during an AES twang exercise or watching $H_2$ drop out during an $M_3$ flageolet transition—relying exclusively on concurrent visual screens during complex musical phrases can induce high cognitive load and visual dependency. Longitudinal studies, including investigations by Berglin et al. (2022), indicate that visual feedback yields the strongest long-term skill retention for learners with low initial accuracy when paired with structured self-appraisal cycles.

Consequently, an optimal Singing Assistance Agent operates using a Closed-Loop Terminal Diagnostic Protocol that progresses through three integrated operational phases. During the initial calibration and baseline capture phase, prior to stylistic coaching, the vocalist records a 3-second sustained neutral vowel (/a/) and a full-range glide (a siren from lowest $M_1$ to highest $M_2/M_3$) in an environment meeting the $42\text{ dB}$ SNR standard. The agent's DSP module processes this baseline to establish the singer's fundamental pitch ambitus, natural passaggio transition frequencies, baseline $H_1 - H_2$ differential, and healthy $\text{CPPS}$ ($\ge 14.47\text{ dB}$) and $\text{AVQI}$ ($\le 2.35$) benchmarks.

Once calibrated, the system enters the dual-modality decomposition phase whenever the user submits a practice recording alongside a coaching query—such as asking whether to approach a high rock chorus with a lighter or heavier mechanism and requesting a targeted exercise. The pipeline renders a log-scale `viridis` narrow-band spectrogram complete with smoothed spectral envelope and LTAS overlays while simultaneously extracting the numerical DSP parameter vector. In the final structured Desp-CoT execution phase, the multimodal agent inspects the 2D spectrogram image alongside in-context reference spectrograms to synthesize a four-part pedagogical response. First, it performs visual and quantitative verification by pinpointing the exact timestamp of vocal instability on the 2D graph—for example, identifying that at $t = 2.4\text{ s}$ on pitch A4, $H_1 - H_2$ drops to $-4.2\text{ dB}$ while inter-harmonic noise spikes, proving pressed $M_1$ hyper-adduction rather than a balanced belt. Second, it executes biomechanical translation by mapping that acoustic anomaly to its anatomical root cause via the Estill and MEAD frameworks, diagnosing that the singer is dragging thick TA vocal fold mass upward with constricted false vocal folds instead of tilting the thyroid or cricoid cartilages and narrowing the epilarynx. Third, it issues an explicit directional weight directive, instructing the singer to adjust toward a lighter, thinner vocal fold edge at the glottal source while increasing supraglottal twang and torso anchoring so the radiated output remains powerful without glottal strain. Finally, the agent prescribes the corresponding physical map and progressive drill from its reference database, establishing an objective visual target on the subsequent spectrogram recording to verify long-term motor acquisition.
